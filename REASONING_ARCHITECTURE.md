## PART I — THE NATURE OF REASONING

*What reasoning is, why it exists, how it differs from all adjacent processes, and why it is the central intelligence layer.*

---

### 1 — What Is Reasoning?

Reasoning is the deliberate, systematic transformation of observations, evidence, and accumulated knowledge into new beliefs, inferences, and justified conclusions. It is the cognitive act of moving intentionally from what is known to what can be inferred — and from what is inferred to what should be decided.

Four essential properties distinguish reasoning from all adjacent cognitive processes:

**Deliberateness** — Reasoning is intentional. It begins when an intelligent agent directs cognitive resources toward a specific question. It does not happen automatically in response to stimulus (that is reflex); it is not the result of pattern-matching alone (that is recognition); it is the active, directed application of intelligence to a problem.

**Directionality** — Reasoning has a logical direction: from inputs (observations, evidence, priors) toward outputs (new beliefs, conclusions, justified decisions). This directionality is traceable. Every output should be traceable to the inputs that produced it and the reasoning steps that connected them.

**Justifiability** — Every conclusion reached through reasoning must be defensible. "I think this is true because X, Y, and Z — weighted as follows — lead to this conclusion" is the signature of reasoned belief. A conclusion that cannot be justified is either intuition, guesswork, or computation — not reasoning.

**Revisability** — Reasoning conclusions are provisional. They represent the best available inference given current evidence. New evidence can and must revise them. A system that cannot update conclusions under new evidence is not reasoning — it is dogmatizing.

In the Investment Intelligence context, reasoning is the act of transforming market data, company disclosures, macroeconomic events, alternative signals, and prior learned knowledge into justified beliefs about prices, companies, economies, and portfolios — and ultimately into justified investment decisions that can be explained, defended, and improved.

**Reasoning is not:**
- A statistical model — that is a tool used within a reasoning process
- A search algorithm — that is one component of reasoning
- An optimization routine — that is the final step after reasoning has determined the objective
- An execution system — that implements what reasoning has concluded
- A database query — that retrieves what reasoning needs to examine
- A machine learning model — that recognizes patterns which reasoning then interprets

**Reasoning is the intelligence itself** — the layer that gives meaning to all the above.

### 3 — Why Reasoning Exists

Reasoning is not an optional enhancement to an intelligence system — it is the mandatory bridge between data and judgment. Five fundamental reasons explain why reasoning must exist:

**Reason 1 — The World Is Uncertain**
No investment decision is made with perfect information. The future is genuinely unknown. Earnings could surprise in either direction. Macroeconomic conditions can shift. Reasoning is the mechanism by which an intelligence system operates constructively under uncertainty — assigning probabilities, weighing evidence, and making justified decisions despite incomplete knowledge.

**Reason 2 — Data Alone Cannot Decide**
Raw data has no direction and no preference. "NIFTY fell 3.2% today" is a fact. Whether to buy, hold, or sell requires reasoning about: why it fell, whether the cause is transient or structural, what similar situations produced in the past, what the portfolio's current risk exposure is, and what the expected outcome of each option is. Data provides inputs; reasoning produces outputs.

**Reason 3 — Evidence Conflicts**
In any complex domain, evidence sources contradict each other. Analyst A rates a stock Buy; Analyst B rates it Sell. Technical signals are bullish; fundamentals are bearish. Macro tailwinds exist alongside company-specific headwinds. Reasoning is the mechanism for resolving conflicts — weighing evidence, assigning credibility, finding coherence, and producing a net conclusion.

**Reason 4 — Actions Must Be Justified**
An intelligence system that cannot explain why it took an action cannot improve when the action fails. Reasoning creates the trace from conclusion back to evidence — the audit trail of thought. This trace is what makes learning possible: "the action failed because the evidence for X was insufficient; I assigned too much weight to Y."

**Reason 5 — Intelligence Must Generalize**
A pure lookup table can match past situations to past actions. But markets generate novel situations continuously. Reasoning is the mechanism by which an intelligence system applies principles learned in past situations to genuinely new situations — generalizing from the known to the unknown. Without reasoning, there is only pattern-matching, which fails at novelty.

### 6 — Why Reasoning Is the Central Intelligence Layer

Reasoning sits at layers 3-6 of the intelligence hierarchy. Everything below it (information, observation, evidence) flows into it. Everything above it (judgement, decision, learning) flows out of it. Reasoning is the essential transformation layer — without it, data cannot become decisions, and outcomes cannot become knowledge.

**The Central Layer Argument:**

```
Without Reasoning:
    Data → [Black Box] → Decision
    ↑ Opaque. Cannot explain. Cannot improve.

With Reasoning:
    Data → Observation → Evidence → Inference → Belief → Judgement → Decision
    ↑ Transparent. Explainable. Auditable. Improvable.
```

Reasoning is also the layer where bias is introduced — and where bias can be detected and corrected. A system that bypasses the reasoning layer cannot correct its own errors because it cannot inspect them. The entire architecture of explainable, improvable investment intelligence depends on reasoning being explicit, structured, and auditable.

### Group D — Probabilistic and Bayesian Concepts
*The vocabulary of uncertainty — how beliefs are quantified and updated under incomplete information.*

| Concept | Definition | Role in Investment Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Probability | A measure of the likelihood of a proposition being true, expressed as 0 to 1 | All beliefs are held at a probability; binary true/false is not permitted |
| Prior Probability | The probability assigned to a hypothesis before observing new evidence | Every hypothesis begins with a calibrated prior based on base rates |
| Posterior Probability | The revised probability assigned after updating on new evidence | The result of every Bayesian reasoning step |
| Likelihood | P(Evidence | Hypothesis) — the probability of observing this evidence if the hypothesis is true | The key Bayesian update factor |
| Conditional Probability | P(A|B) — the probability of A being true given that B is known to be true | All investment probabilities are conditional on the current regime, context, and evidence |
| Joint Probability | P(A and B) — the probability that both A and B are true simultaneously | Used in multi-hypothesis reasoning |
| Marginal Probability | P(A) — the probability of A regardless of the state of any other variable | The base rate of an event in the overall reference class |
| Bayes' Theorem | Posterior = (Likelihood × Prior) / Evidence Probability — the fundamental update rule | The core mechanism of the Bayesian reasoning engine |
| Bayesian Update | The process of revising a prior probability using Bayes' Theorem upon receiving new evidence | Applied at every new evidence arrival |
| Prior Distribution | A probability distribution over all possible values of a hypothesis before evidence | Encodes the system's pre-evidence uncertainty about a continuous quantity (e.g., earnings range) |
| Posterior Distribution | The updated probability distribution after evidence has been applied | The system's post-evidence uncertainty estimate |
| Likelihood Ratio | The ratio of P(Evidence|H1) to P(Evidence|H0) — quantifies how much evidence favors H1 over H0 | A likelihood ratio > 1 favors H1; < 1 favors H0; > 10 is strong evidence |
| Odds | A ratio representation of probability: Odds(A) = P(A) / P(not-A) | Alternative to probability for Bayesian calculations |
| Posterior Odds | Prior Odds × Likelihood Ratio — the multiplicative Bayesian update rule | More intuitive than the probability form for multi-evidence updating |
| Calibration | The alignment between stated probability and actual frequency of events | A system that says "70% confident" should be right 70% of the time |
| Frequentist Probability | Probability defined as the long-run frequency of an event in repeated trials | Used for historical pattern analysis and base rate estimation |
| Bayesian Probability | Probability defined as a rational degree of belief given evidence — subjective but well-defined | The primary framework for investment conviction quantification |
| Subjective Probability | A probability estimate based on personal judgment in the absence of sufficient data | Permitted only when base rates are unavailable; must be declared as subjective |
| Objective Probability | A probability derived from sufficient observational data | Preferred; quantifies historical base rates |
| Calibration Error | The degree of systematic miscalibration in probability estimates | Tracked and corrected by the learning system |
| Overconfidence | Probability estimates that are too extreme — too close to 0 or 1 | Primary calibration failure; requires active detection |
| Underconfidence | Probability estimates that are too moderate — too close to 0.5 | Prevents the system from acting on genuine evidence |
| Expected Value | The probability-weighted average of all possible outcomes | The decision-theoretic basis for trade sizing and selection |
| Variance | The spread of possible outcomes around the expected value | Captures risk; used in position sizing |
| Entropy | A measure of uncertainty — maximum entropy means maximum uncertainty | Used to quantify the information content of a signal |
| Information Gain | The reduction in uncertainty (entropy) from observing a new piece of evidence | Measures how valuable a piece of evidence is |
| Base Rate | The prior frequency of an event in the relevant reference class | Must be established before evaluating specific evidence |
| Base Rate Neglect | Ignoring the base rate when interpreting specific evidence | Critical failure mode; specific vivid evidence feels more relevant than base rate statistics |
| Regression to the Mean | Extreme values in a measured variable tend to move back toward the average over time | "Exceptionally strong earnings will probably be followed by more moderate growth" |
| Law of Large Numbers | The sample mean converges to the true mean as sample size grows | Justifies pattern-based investment knowledge items when sample is large |
| Confidence Interval | A range that contains the true value with a specified probability | All forecasts should include a confidence interval |
| Credible Interval | A Bayesian range — the system believes the true value lies within this range with stated probability | More appropriate than frequentist confidence intervals for single-case investment decisions |
| Effect Size | The magnitude of an effect, independent of statistical significance and sample size | Large effect + statistical significance = actionable knowledge |
| Statistical Power | The probability of detecting a true effect when it exists | Low-power tests miss real patterns — increases false negative rate |
| Type I Error | Rejecting a true null hypothesis — acting on a false signal | The false positive error; wasted resources and opportunity cost |
| Type II Error | Failing to reject a false null hypothesis — ignoring a true signal | The false negative error; missed opportunity |
| Bayesian Network | A probabilistic graphical model representing conditional dependencies between variables | The structural backbone of multi-factor evidence integration |
| Monte Carlo Simulation | Using random sampling to estimate the probability distribution of outcomes | Used in scenario analysis and risk estimation |
| Bootstrap Sampling | Resampling from existing data to estimate sampling distributions | Enables statistical inference with limited historical data |
| Ergodicity | The property that time-average equals ensemble-average — not all systems are ergodic | Investment returns are non-ergodic; expected value calculations alone are insufficient |
| Fat Tails | Probability distributions with more extreme events than a normal distribution | All financial return distributions have fat tails; must be explicitly modeled |
| Black Swan | A highly improbable, high-impact event outside normal expectation | Explicit modeling category; the kill switch is designed for black swans |

### Group E — Cognitive Bias Concepts
*The vocabulary of systematic reasoning errors — the enemies of accurate belief formation.*

| Concept | Definition | Role in Investment Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Cognitive Bias | A systematic, predictable pattern of deviation from rationality in judgment | The collective enemy — the system must actively detect and correct for all known biases |
| Confirmation Bias | The tendency to seek, interpret, and remember information that confirms existing beliefs | The most dangerous investment bias; drives the mandatory contradiction-seeking protocol |
| Disconfirmation Bias | Applying stronger scrutiny to evidence that contradicts existing beliefs | The asymmetric partner of confirmation bias; equally dangerous |
| Availability Heuristic | Judging the probability of events by how easily examples come to mind | "The most recent market crash feels like the most likely scenario" |
| Representativeness Heuristic | Judging probability by how closely something matches a prototype | "This company looks like Google in 2005 — must be a buy" |
| Anchoring Bias | Excessive reliance on the first piece of information received | Price anchoring: "The stock was ₹500 last year; ₹350 feels cheap" |
| Adjustment Bias | Insufficient adjustment from an anchor when new information arrives | Prevents proper Bayesian updates; estimates stay too close to the anchor |
| Recency Bias | Over-weighting recent events relative to the full historical pattern | "The market has gone up for 3 months — of course it will continue" |
| Survivorship Bias | Analyzing only successful cases while ignoring failures | "All great investors concentrated — I should concentrate" ignores the failed concentrators |
| Hindsight Bias | Believing after the fact that the outcome was predictable | Corrupts learning: prevents the system from understanding its actual pre-event uncertainty |
| Narrative Bias | Preferring coherent story-consistent explanations over less coherent but statistically better ones | "This company tells a great story" overrides poor fundamentals |
| Attribution Bias | Incorrectly attributing outcomes to the wrong causes | "We won because of skill" when it was luck — prevents proper strategy evaluation |
| Self-Attribution Bias | Crediting oneself for successes while blaming external forces for failures | Creates an asymmetric learning record that overestimates strategy quality |
| Overconfidence Bias | Excessive confidence in one's own knowledge, predictions, or abilities | The primary epistemic failure mode; tracked by calibration metrics |
| Planning Fallacy | Systematically underestimating time, cost, and difficulty of complex tasks | Affects trade timeline estimates and recovery scenario projections |
| Sunk Cost Fallacy | Continuing to commit resources because of past investment, despite poor current prospects | "I cannot sell at a loss" — the capital allocation version |
| Gambler's Fallacy | Believing past independent events influence future independent events | "The market has fallen 5 days in a row — it must reverse tomorrow" |
| Hot Hand Fallacy | Believing a current streak implies continuation beyond statistical expectation | "This strategy has been profitable for 10 trades — it's on a roll" |
| Illusion of Control | Believing one has greater influence over outcomes than is actually the case | Investment version: over-trading and over-managing positions |
| Status Quo Bias | Preference for the current state over alternatives, even when alternatives are better | "We've always done it this way" — resists systematic improvement |
| Loss Aversion Bias | Losses feel approximately 2× as painful as equivalent gains feel pleasurable | Causes asymmetric decision-making; holding losing positions too long |
| Framing Effect | Identical information presented differently elicits different decisions | "90% survival rate" vs "10% mortality rate" — same fact, different response |
| Halo Effect | Letting one positive attribute create unduly positive impressions of unrelated attributes | "Management is brilliant at operations; therefore their capital allocation must also be brilliant" |
| Horn Effect | Letting one negative attribute create unduly negative impressions overall | One bad quarter creates a negative lens through which all subsequent data is filtered |
| Authority Bias | Over-weighting the opinion of perceived authorities regardless of evidence | "Goldman Sachs rates it a buy — must be worth considering carefully" |
| Bandwagon Effect | Adopting beliefs because many others hold them — social proof | Reinforces crowded trades; undermines independent analysis |
| Group Think | Suppression of individual dissent in favor of group harmony | Primary risk in multi-agent debate if the debate process is not properly structured |
| False Consensus Effect | Over-estimating how widely one's views are shared in the broader market | Creates surprise when the expected consensus moves is not realized |
| Illusion of Validity | Excessive confidence in predictions based on internally consistent but poorly-grounded narratives | A compelling narrative that "makes sense" feels more certain than it should |
| Clustering Illusion | Seeing meaningful patterns in truly random data — false pattern recognition | The system must validate patterns against statistical null hypotheses |
| Scope Insensitivity | Similar emotional reactions to problems of very different magnitudes | ₹10 crore loss feels similar to ₹100 crore loss emotionally — calibration deficit |
| Information Cascade | Adopting the decisions of others even when one's own private information suggests differently | Creates herding; the independent evidence check prevents this |
| Attentional Bias | Paying disproportionate attention to emotionally salient information | Vivid recent news dominates analytically superior but less vivid historical data |
| Commitment Bias | Reluctance to abandon a position once a public or firm commitment has been made | The debating system must allow position changes without penalty |
| Contrast Effect | Judging something relative to what was seen immediately before | Price anchoring: "₹120 seems cheap after looking at ₹200 stocks" |
| Mental Accounting | Treating money differently based on its source or designated purpose | "This is house money" — all capital is equally valuable regardless of origin |
| Outcome Bias | Judging the quality of a decision by its outcome rather than by the quality of the process | Dangerous for learning: a good decision with a bad outcome should be rated as a good decision |

### Group G — Confidence and Conviction Concepts
*The vocabulary of belief strength — how certainty is earned, quantified, and evolved.*

| Concept | Definition | Role in Investment Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Confidence | A probability estimate of a belief being correct — the numerical measure of belief strength | Every belief and hypothesis carries a confidence score between 0 and 1 |
| Conviction | A high-confidence belief backed by multiple independent, converging evidence streams — above the action threshold | Conviction is earned, not assumed; it is the pre-condition for a decision |
| Epistemic Confidence | Confidence based on the quality and quantity of evidence | The primary form; grows with evidence accumulation |
| Aleatory Confidence | Confidence about the inherent randomness in an outcome — irreducible uncertainty | Cannot be eliminated; only measured and bounded |
| Calibrated Confidence | Confidence that accurately reflects true probability — 70% confident means right 70% of the time | The target state; the learning system calibrates toward this |
| Conviction Threshold | The minimum conviction score required to trigger a decision recommendation | Set at 6.5/10 in the current system; below this, no action recommendation |
| Conviction Score | A composite score aggregating evidence weight, source quality, convergence, and contradiction discount | The system's quantified investment conviction for a specific thesis |
| Prior Confidence | The confidence in a hypothesis before any new evidence for this specific instance | Initialized from base rates and historical performance |
| Posterior Confidence | The updated confidence after applying all available evidence | The current confidence; updated continuously as new evidence arrives |
| Evidence Convergence Bonus | The increase in conviction when multiple independent evidence streams agree | Independent confirmation is the most powerful conviction builder |
| Contradiction Penalty | The reduction in conviction when high-quality contradicting evidence is discovered | Contradictions must reduce conviction — ignoring them is a failure mode |
| Source Credibility Weight | The multiplier applied to evidence based on the reliability track record of the source | High-credibility sources earn higher weight through demonstrated accuracy |
| Novelty Bonus | A small conviction increment for genuinely new types of evidence not seen before | Novel evidence carries information that updates the system's world model |
| Recency Weight | The degree to which recent evidence is weighted more than older evidence | Decays with time; older evidence is discounted by a staleness factor |
| Confidence Floor | The minimum confidence level below which a hypothesis is archived rather than held | No active hypothesis may be held with confidence below 10% |
| Confidence Ceiling | The maximum confidence level permitted for any investment hypothesis | No hypothesis may be held at 100%; uncertainty is constitutionally required |
| Confidence Decay | The natural decrease in confidence as time passes and evidence ages | All beliefs decay toward the prior without new evidence |
| Confidence Growth | The increase in confidence from receiving new corroborating evidence | Grows faster with more surprising evidence (higher surprise value) |
| Confidence Gradient | The rate of change in confidence over time in a specific direction | Steep positive gradient = rapidly building case; steep negative = rapidly collapsing thesis |
| Agreement Score | The proportion of evidence items that support the hypothesis | Calculated as: supporting evidence count / total evidence count |
| Contradiction Score | The proportion of evidence items that contradict the hypothesis | Calculated as: contradicting evidence count / total evidence count |
| Conviction Momentum | The trend in conviction over time — is the case strengthening or weakening? | A weakening conviction trend is an early warning even before threshold breach |
| Reliability | The consistency with which a source or method produces accurate signals over time | The track record score; updated after every outcome |
| Trust | The compound measure of reliability + alignment + transparency of a reasoning partner | Applied to external analysts, data sources, and internal AI agents |
| Uncertainty | The honest measure of what is not known — the complement of confidence | Must be explicitly quantified; suppressing uncertainty is a failure mode |
| Ambiguity | A special form of uncertainty where the probability distribution itself is unknown | The highest form of uncertainty; requires explicit acknowledgment |
| Risk | Uncertainty combined with exposure — the possibility of loss from holding a belief that could be wrong | Confidence must be balanced against the cost of being wrong |
| Doubt | A specific low-confidence challenge to a particular element of a hypothesis | Healthy doubt drives investigation; excessive doubt prevents action |
| Skepticism | A disciplined questioning stance toward all evidence and conclusions | The system's default attitude toward any new claim |
| Credulity | Accepting claims without sufficient scrutiny | The opposite of skepticism; a primary reasoning failure mode |

### Group H — Decision Concepts

| Concept | Definition | Role in Investment Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Decision | A committed choice from among alternatives that allocates resources and produces consequences | The terminal product of the reasoning pipeline; the system's output |
| Decision Maker | The agent or system authorized to commit to a course of action | The DecisionEngine; subject to RiskGuardian approval |
| Decision Criteria | The explicit standards against which alternatives are evaluated | Must be defined before alternatives are evaluated; prevents post-hoc rationalization |
| Decision Rule | A formal specification of how to map from evidence states to decision outputs | Example: "If conviction ≥ 6.5 and VIX < 30 and portfolio utilization < 85%, generate recommendation" |
| Option | A distinct possible course of action available to the decision maker | Buy / Sell / Hold / Increase / Reduce / Hedge |
| Trade-off | The cost of choosing one option is foregoing another | All decisions involve trade-offs; they must be made explicit |
| Expected Utility | The probability-weighted average utility across all possible outcomes of a decision | The decision-theoretic basis for rational choice |
| Risk-Adjusted Return | Expected return modified by a risk measure — the investment analogue of expected utility | Sharpe Ratio, Sortino Ratio, Calmar Ratio |
| Decision Quality | The quality of a decision judged by the process, not the outcome | A good process that leads to a bad outcome is still a quality decision |
| Decision Speed | The time from evidence arrival to decision production | Faster decisions reduce opportunity cost; slower decisions reduce noise sensitivity |
| Decision Confidence | The conviction of the decision maker in the correctness of a decision | Must be recorded at time of decision for later calibration analysis |
| Reversibility | Whether a decision can be undone if new evidence changes the assessment | Reversible decisions carry lower decisional risk and permit more rapid action |
| Commitment | The point at which a decision becomes binding and action begins | Orders become commitments; prior to that, everything is provisional |
| Satisficing | Selecting an option that is "good enough" rather than searching for the theoretical optimum | Appropriate when search cost > marginal gain from finding the optimal |
| Maximizing | Searching for the optimal option across all alternatives | Appropriate when the marginal gain is large and the search cost is manageable |
| Opportunity Cost | The value of the best foregone alternative | Every decision implicitly rejects all other options; their value must be considered |
| Regret | The negative feeling from choosing a worse option when a better option was available | Regret minimization is a useful decision framework for irreversible decisions |
| Decision Under Certainty | Choosing when all outcomes are known | Extremely rare in investment; exists only for scheduled events with defined payoffs |
| Decision Under Risk | Choosing when outcome probabilities are known or estimable | The normal state; risk models provide probabilities |
| Decision Under Uncertainty | Choosing when outcome probabilities cannot be reliably estimated | Applies to black swan events and regime transitions |
| Decision Under Ambiguity | Choosing when neither outcomes nor probabilities are known | The hardest case; requires robust strategies that survive many scenarios |
| Decision Fatigue | The deterioration of decision quality from making too many decisions | The system avoids by filtering to only the highest-quality decision opportunities |
| Overrides | Decisions that override the system's recommendation — human or risk-guardian intervention | All overrides are logged; patterns of override reveal system weaknesses |
| Escalation | Referring a decision to a higher authority when normal criteria are insufficient | Protocol for extreme events: kill switch decisions go directly to RiskGuardian |
| Anchoring in Decision | Excessive attachment to a prior decision that prevents appropriate updating | "I entered at ₹1,000; I cannot sell at ₹900" |
| Decision Log | The permanent record of every decision: rationale, evidence, confidence, outcome | The audit trail; the foundation of the learning system |
| Sub-optimal Decision | A decision that is valid but not the best available given the information | Acceptable; the system targets consistently good, not occasionally perfect |
| Dominant Strategy | An option that is better than all alternatives in every possible scenario | The ideal situation; rare in practice |
| Dominated Strategy | An option that is worse than at least one alternative in every possible scenario | Never choose; immediately eliminated |
| Pareto Optimality | A state where no participant can be made better off without making another worse off | The efficiency frontier for portfolio construction |

| Valuation | The estimation of the intrinsic value of a company or asset | The anchor for fundamental investment thesis construction |
| Re-rating | A change in the valuation multiple applied to a company or sector | The primary driver of medium-term returns distinct from earnings growth |
| Earnings Revision | A change in analyst consensus estimates for future earnings | One of the highest-weight evidence types for short-term price prediction |
| Price Discovery | The process by which markets determine the price of an asset | The system participates in and benefits from this process |

| Factor Investing | Systematic exposure to documented risk premia — value, momentum, quality, size | The conceptual framework for evidence-based systematic strategy construction |

### Group L — Reasoning Failure Mode Concepts
*The vocabulary of how reasoning breaks down — the comprehensive taxonomy of reasoning errors.*

| Concept | Definition | Role in Investment Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Reasoning Failure | Any systematic error in the reasoning process that produces unjustified or incorrect conclusions | The system must monitor for all known failure modes continuously |
| Overconfidence | Assigning confidence levels that exceed what the evidence justifies | The most common and costly failure mode; tracked by calibration metrics |
| Hallucination | Generating conclusions that are not supported by any actual evidence | Must be prevented by requiring evidence citation for all conclusions |
| Noise Amplification | Treating random variation as meaningful signal | Addressed by statistical validation requirements for all patterns |
| False Correlation | Concluding that two variables are causally related because they are correlated | Correlation is not causation; all correlations require mechanism validation |
| Spurious Causality | Identifying a causal relationship where only correlation or coincidence exists | Causal claims require causal mechanism evidence, not just statistical association |
| Data Leakage | Using information in a model that would not have been available at the time of decision | The primary backtesting failure mode; all validation is strictly point-in-time |
| Lookahead Bias | Using future information to evaluate a past decision | Corrupts all historical analysis; prevented by time-stamped data architecture |
| Survivorship Bias in Analysis | Analyzing only companies or strategies that still exist | Addressed by requiring complete universe data including failures |
| Information Cascade | Abandoning well-supported private beliefs in favor of a perceived public consensus | Prevented by the independent evidence requirement before consensus is considered |
| Circular Logic | Using a conclusion as a premise in the argument that produces it | Detected by the argument map validation step |
| Premature Conclusion | Reaching a conclusion before sufficient evidence has been assembled | Prevented by the conviction threshold requirement |
| Incomplete Evidence | Making a high-confidence conclusion from a partial evidence set | The completeness check at the end of each reasoning cycle prevents this |
| Hidden Assumptions | Conclusions that depend on unstated and unverified assumptions | All assumptions must be made explicit and validated |
| Model Drift | A model's performance degrading as the market environment changes | Detected by continuous performance monitoring; triggers retraining |
| Regime Blindness | Applying strategies calibrated for one regime to a fundamentally different regime | The regime context check is mandatory before every decision |
| Black Swan Blindness | Ignoring the possibility of extreme events outside historical experience | Explicitly addressed by the kill switch and fat-tail scenario modeling |
| Selection Bias | Systematically including only a non-representative subset of evidence | Requires complete evidence collection before reasoning begins |
| Narrative Consistency Bias | Selecting evidence that fits the prevailing narrative and dismissing evidence that doesn't | The contradiction-seeking protocol is specifically designed to counteract this |
| Recency Overweighting | Assigning excessive influence to the most recent evidence | The staleness decay function is calibrated to prevent this |
| Anchoring in Analysis | Insufficient adjustment from an initial estimate when new evidence arrives | Prevented by the Bayesian update requirement: all priors are re-estimated, not adjusted |
| Confirmation Cascade | Each piece of confirming evidence inflating confidence beyond what a strict Bayesian calculation would produce | Addressed by the independence check: dependent evidence streams are not double-counted |
| Base Rate Neglect | Ignoring the historical frequency of an event when evaluating specific evidence | Base rate is always the required starting point for probability estimation |
| Expert Overreliance | Over-weighting analyst or expert opinion relative to primary evidence | Expert opinion is one evidence type among many; it receives a defined weight, not deference |
| Explainability Gap | Conclusions that cannot be traced back to the evidence and reasoning steps that produced them | Every conclusion must be explainable; black-box conclusions are rejected |
| Premature Optimization | Finding the best trade implementation before validating the underlying thesis | Thesis quality precedes execution optimization |
| Category Error | Applying reasoning from one category of events to a different, fundamentally different category | Prevented by the event classification requirement |
| Temporal Confusion | Conflating evidence from different time periods | All evidence is time-stamped; temporal consistency is required |
| Level of Analysis Error | Drawing company-level conclusions from sector-level evidence | Each level of analysis (global, macro, sector, company) must be analyzed separately |

## PART III — REASONING PRIMITIVES

*The foundational units of investment intelligence reasoning. Every reasoning process in the system is built from these primitives. Each is defined with 28 attributes to provide complete architectural specification.*

---

### PRIM-001 — OBSERVATION

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Primitive Code** | PRIM-001 |
| **Name** | Observation |
| **Definition** | The act of registering a specific, concrete, time-stamped occurrence or measurement from the world — the first transformation step from raw information into potentially useful input for reasoning |
| **Why It Exists** | Before any reasoning can occur, the world must be perceived. Observations are the system's connection to external reality. Without observations, the system is reasoning from assumptions alone — which is epistemically unacceptable. |
| **Classification** | Epistemic | Foundational | Input Layer |
| **Inputs Required** | Raw data stream; observation trigger (threshold, schedule, or event); timestamp source |
| **Outputs Produced** | A structured record: what was observed, when, at what level, from which source, with what confidence |
| **Producers** | Data feeds (price data, volume data); news monitoring; financial filing monitors; economic data releases |
| **Consumers** | Evidence classification layer; signal generation; anomaly detection; hypothesis activation |
| **Quality Dimensions** | Accuracy (was it measured correctly?); Precision (how exactly?); Completeness (all aspects captured?); Timeliness (minimal delay?) |
| **Failure Modes** | Failed data feed (observation missing); measurement error (wrong value); timing error (wrong timestamp); scope error (observed irrelevant thing) |
| **Strengths** | Anchors reasoning in reality; prevents reasoning from fiction; creates the audit trail |
| **Weaknesses** | Observations are theory-laden — what we notice depends on what we are looking for; can miss important things outside the scope |
| **Dependencies** | Live data infrastructure; observation criteria definition; timestamp accuracy |
| **Relationships to Other Primitives** | Precedes → Evidence; Generates → Signal (if materially significant); Enters → Working Memory |
| **Lifecycle** | Raw data arrives → Threshold/pattern check → Observation registered → Timestamp attached → Source recorded → Enters evidence queue |
| **Validation Methods** | Cross-source verification; historical comparison; range validation; anomaly check |
| **Confidence Requirements** | Observations carry the reliability score of their source, not additional confidence — they are recorded as-is |
| **State Changes When Created** | Working memory: new observation enters; Event log: recorded; Source feed: consumption logged |
| **Temporal Properties** | Observations are point-in-time; their staleness begins immediately; relevance decays by evidence type |
| **Scope** | From a single tick price to a macro data release — any concrete measurement from the world |
| **Reversibility** | Irreversible — the observation happened; it is permanently recorded |
| **Importance to System** | Critical — the entire reasoning chain originates here; no observations = no intelligence |
| **Risk If Missing** | System reasons from prior beliefs only; becomes disconnected from current market reality |
| **Examples** | "NIFTY is trading at 24,320 at 11:15 IST on 2026-07-01"; "HDFC Q3 EPS = ₹22.15 (filed with BSE at 09:32)"; "India VIX = 28.4 at session open" |
| **Anti-Examples** | "NIFTY is expensive" (that is a judgment, not an observation); "HDFC is doing well" (that is an inference) |
| **Complexity** | Low — observations are structurally simple; complexity lies in knowing what to observe |
| **Evolution Over Time** | Observation scope expands as new data sources are added; observation precision improves with better data infrastructure |

### PRIM-002 — EVIDENCE

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Primitive Code** | PRIM-002 |
| **Name** | Evidence |
| **Definition** | An observation or set of observations that has been evaluated for relevance to a specific hypothesis — and found to materially change the probability of that hypothesis being true or false |
| **Why It Exists** | Raw observations are undifferentiated — not all observations are relevant to all hypotheses. Evidence is the selective, contextual transformation of observations into hypothesis-relevant inputs. It is the first step of interpretation. |
| **Classification** | Epistemic | Interpretive | Hypothesis-Relative |
| **Inputs Required** | An observation (PRIM-001); an active hypothesis (PRIM-004); a relevance assessment function; a probability change estimate |
| **Outputs Produced** | A structured evidence record: the observation, the hypothesis it applies to, the direction (supports/contradicts), the weight, the source reliability score |
| **Producers** | Evidence classification engine; analyst agents; news processing layer |
| **Consumers** | Conviction calculation; hypothesis updating; debate; decision engine |
| **Quality Dimensions** | Relevance (how directly does it relate to the hypothesis?); Reliability (how trustworthy is the source?); Recency (how current is it?); Independence (is it independent of other evidence items?) |
| **Failure Modes** | Misclassification (contradicting evidence classified as supporting); source unreliability; staleness; double-counting dependent evidence |
| **Strengths** | Transforms raw data into actionable hypothesis input; creates the structured basis for probabilistic reasoning |
| **Weaknesses** | Relevance assessment requires human-equivalent understanding; borderline cases are difficult to classify |
| **Dependencies** | Active hypothesis to which evidence applies; source reliability database; evidence weighting model |
| **Relationships to Other Primitives** | Derived from → Observation (PRIM-001); Inputs to → Hypothesis (PRIM-004); Produces → Signal (PRIM-003) when threshold crossed; Feeds → Conviction (PRIM-007) |
| **Lifecycle** | Observation arrives → Hypothesis relevance check → Direction determined (supports/contradicts/neutral) → Weight assigned → Staleness timer starts → Enters hypothesis evidence pool → Decays with time → Archived when hypothesis resolves |
| **Validation Methods** | Relevance score validation; source track record check; independence verification; comparison with prior similar evidence |
| **Confidence Requirements** | Evidence carries a confidence score = Source Reliability × Relevance Score |
| **State Changes When Created** | Hypothesis: prior probability updates; Conviction score: recalculates; Evidence log: entry added |
| **Temporal Properties** | Evidence has a timestamp and a validity period; significance decays with time unless refreshed |
| **Scope** | Hypothesis-relative — evidence is always evidence FOR or AGAINST something specific |
| **Reversibility** | The occurrence is irreversible; the relevance classification can be revised as understanding improves |
| **Importance to System** | Critical — without evidence, conviction cannot be calculated; all reasoning would be purely a priori |
| **Risk If Missing** | Conviction calculations become purely prior-driven; system cannot update on market reality |
| **Examples** | "HDFC Q3 EPS beat of 12% (Weight: 0.85, Direction: Supports Bullish Thesis)"; "RBI rate cut (Weight: 0.90, Direction: Supports Banking Sector Bullish Thesis)"; "Promoter selling 0.8% stake (Weight: 0.75, Direction: Contradicts Bullish Thesis)" |
| **Anti-Examples** | The raw fact "HDFC EPS = ₹22.15" without hypothesis context is an observation, not evidence; "Market is bullish" without a specific hypothesis is not evidence |
| **Complexity** | Medium — classifying evidence correctly requires domain understanding |
| **Evolution Over Time** | Evidence weighting model improves with learning; new evidence types are discovered and added |

### PRIM-003 — SIGNAL

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Primitive Code** | PRIM-003 |
| **Name** | Signal |
| **Definition** | A derived, composite indicator that crosses a defined threshold and directs the system's attention or triggers a specific reasoning process — the attention-management mechanism of the intelligence system |
| **Why It Exists** | The system cannot reason about everything simultaneously. Signals are the filtering mechanism — they indicate which observations and evidence streams deserve active reasoning resources. A signal says: "Pay attention to this now." |
| **Classification** | Attention Management | Derived | Threshold-Crossing |
| **Inputs Required** | One or more observations or evidence items; a signal definition (criteria and threshold); a baseline for comparison |
| **Outputs Produced** | A binary or graded signal fire event with: signal type, magnitude, direction, timestamp, underlying evidence, confidence |
| **Producers** | Technical analysis engine; fundamental monitoring; macro watchlist; options chain monitor; AI anomaly detector |
| **Consumers** | Hypothesis generator (new hypothesis initiated or existing one reinforced); Alert system (operator notification); Conviction calculator |
| **Quality Dimensions** | Precision (low false positive rate); Recall (low false negative rate); Latency (how quickly does it fire after the underlying event?); Persistence (does it stay on long enough to act?) |
| **Failure Modes** | False positive (fires when no real development); false negative (misses real development); premature fire (fires too early); late fire (fires too late to act) |
| **Strengths** | Filters the attention space; enables real-time response; quantifiable performance |
| **Weaknesses** | All signals have non-zero false rates; threshold setting is an optimization challenge |
| **Dependencies** | Observation infrastructure; signal definition library; threshold calibration |
| **Relationships to Other Primitives** | Derived from → Evidence (PRIM-002) or Observations (PRIM-001); Initiates or Reinforces → Hypothesis (PRIM-004); Enters → Conviction (PRIM-007) |
| **Lifecycle** | Monitoring criteria active → Data arrives → Criteria evaluated → Threshold crossed → Signal fires → Signal logged → Hypothesis triggered or reinforced → Signal decays if not reinforced → Expires |
| **Validation Methods** | Historical false positive/negative rate tracking; signal precision/recall metrics; calibration against outcomes |
| **Confidence Requirements** | Signal confidence = signal type base accuracy × current evidence quality |
| **State Changes When Created** | Alert queue: entry added; Hypothesis: new created or existing reinforced; Monitoring: intensity increased |
| **Temporal Properties** | Signals have a fire time and a validity duration; many signals are session-level; some persist days |
| **Scope** | From a single-stock breakout to a market-wide risk-off signal |
| **Reversibility** | The signal event is irreversible; the assessment of its validity updates as outcomes are known |
| **Importance to System** | High — the primary attention-allocation mechanism; without signals, the system would have to reason about everything |
| **Risk If Missing** | Information overload; inability to prioritize reasoning resources; delayed response to important developments |
| **Examples** | "TATASTEEL breakout signal: price closed above 6-month resistance on 4× volume (Confidence: 0.78, Direction: Bull, Fired: 14:23 IST)"; "VIX spike signal: VIX > 30 threshold crossed (Confidence: 0.95, Type: Risk-Off, Immediate)" |
| **Anti-Examples** | "TATASTEEL looks interesting" is not a signal — it has no defined threshold, no measurable criteria |
| **Complexity** | Low to Medium per signal; high across the full signal library |
| **Evolution Over Time** | New signal types added from learning; thresholds recalibrated quarterly |

| **Primitive Code** | PRIM-004 |
| **Name** | Hypothesis |

| **Why It Exists** | Hypotheses provide the structure within which evidence is accumulated and reasoning occurs. Without a hypothesis, evidence has no anchor — it cannot be accumulated coherently or used to produce justified conclusions. The hypothesis is the organizing center of investment reasoning. |
| **Classification** | Epistemic | Organizational | Central |
| **Inputs Required** | Initial signal (PRIM-003); relevant entity; directional claim; timeframe; expected magnitude; key assumptions; initial prior probability |
| **Outputs Produced** | A structured hypothesis record: the claim, the entity, the timeframe, the evidence pool, the conviction score, the key assumptions |
| **Producers** | Signal → Hypothesis generator; Opportunit Engine; Multi-agent debate initiator |
| **Consumers** | All reasoning agents (receive hypothesis as input); Conviction calculator; Decision engine; Learning system (receives outcome) |
| **Quality Dimensions** | Falsifiability (can it be proven wrong?); Specificity (is the claim precise?); Completeness (are all key factors considered?); Independence (is it distinct from existing hypotheses?) |

| **Strengths** | Creates the organizing structure for evidence accumulation; enables systematic confirmation and falsification |
| **Weaknesses** | Hypothesis creation is prone to confirmation bias — we create hypotheses that fit our existing views |
| **Dependencies** | Initial signal; entity definition; prior probability from base rates |
| **Relationships to Other Primitives** | Created by → Signal (PRIM-003); Accumulates → Evidence (PRIM-002); Produces → Inference (PRIM-005); Receives → Belief (PRIM-006); Terminates → Decision (PRIM-009) or Archival |

| **Validation Methods** | Falsifiability test; specificity review; assumption audit; contradiction check |
| **Confidence Requirements** | Hypothesis begins with prior probability from base rates; updates with each evidence item |

| **Temporal Properties** | Hypotheses have a creation time, an expected resolution time, and a maximum validity period |
| **Scope** | A single hypothesis spans one investment opportunity — one entity, one direction, one timeframe |
| **Reversibility** | A hypothesis can be revised as evidence updates; it is archived (not deleted) when resolved |
| **Importance to System** | Critical — the organizing unit of all investment intelligence |
| **Risk If Missing** | Evidence accumulates without structure; reasoning has no focus; conviction cannot be calculated |
| **Examples** | "HDFC Bank Q4 EPS will beat consensus by >5% driven by NIM expansion (Confidence: 62%, Timeframe: 45 days, Key Assumption: NIM expands 15bps in Q4)"; "NIFTY will fall >5% within 30 days if FII outflow exceeds ₹5,000 crore in 10 sessions" |
| **Anti-Examples** | "HDFC Bank is a good company" — not falsifiable, no timeframe, no magnitude; "The market might go up or down" — trivially true |
| **Complexity** | Medium — creating a well-formed hypothesis requires domain knowledge and discipline |
| **Evolution Over Time** | Hypothesis templates improve; alternative hypothesis generation becomes more sophisticated |

### PRIM-005 — INFERENCE

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Primitive Code** | PRIM-005 |
| **Name** | Inference |
| **Definition** | A new belief derived from existing beliefs and evidence through a specified reasoning process — the act of going beyond the evidence to what the evidence implies |
| **Why It Exists** | Inference is the generative act of intelligence. Direct observation gives us facts; inference gives us understanding. We cannot observe the future — we can only infer it. We cannot observe the causes of events — we can only infer them. Inference is what transforms a fact-recorder into an intelligence system. |
| **Classification** | Epistemic | Generative | Productive |
| **Inputs Required** | Premises (established beliefs or observations); a reasoning process (deductive, inductive, abductive, Bayesian); an inferential rule |
| **Outputs Produced** | A new belief proposition with a confidence level, a reasoning trace, and a declaration of which reasoning type was used |
| **Producers** | All reasoning agents; the inference engine |
| **Consumers** | Hypothesis evidence pool (inference becomes evidence for a higher-level hypothesis); Belief system; Decision engine |
| **Quality Dimensions** | Validity (does the conclusion follow from the premises?); Soundness (are the premises justified?); Explainability (is the reasoning traceable?); Conservatism (does it not over-reach the evidence?) |
| **Failure Modes** | Invalid inference (conclusion does not follow); unsound inference (false premises); over-inference (conclusion goes beyond what premises justify); circular inference (conclusion in premises) |
| **Strengths** | The generative power of intelligence; enables prediction, explanation, and understanding beyond observation |
| **Weaknesses** | Can be wrong; must be held provisionally; the more steps from the evidence, the higher the accumulated error |
| **Dependencies** | Valid premises; applicable reasoning rule; domain knowledge to identify the appropriate inference type |
| **Relationships to Other Primitives** | Derived from → Evidence (PRIM-002) + Beliefs (PRIM-006) + Knowledge Items; Enters → New Belief (PRIM-006); Feeds → Conviction (PRIM-007); Can become → Evidence for higher-level hypothesis |
| **Lifecycle** | Evidence assembled → Reasoning type selected → Inferential rule applied → New belief produced → Confidence assigned → Reasoning trace recorded → Belief enters the belief pool → Updated or replaced by stronger evidence |
| **Validation Methods** | Logical validity check; assumption audit; historical accuracy of this inference type in similar situations |
| **Confidence Requirements** | Inference confidence = product of premise confidences × inferential rule reliability |
| **State Changes When Created** | Belief pool: new entry; Hypothesis conviction: may update; Reasoning trace: logged |
| **Temporal Properties** | Inferences are valid as long as their premises remain valid; expire when premises are updated |
| **Scope** | From a simple single-step implication to a complex multi-step chain |
| **Reversibility** | The inference event is permanent (recorded); the resulting belief is revisable |
| **Importance to System** | Critical — the mechanism by which the system produces new understanding |
| **Risk If Missing** | System can only report facts; cannot generate predictions, explanations, or forward-looking judgments |
| **Examples** | "PREMISE: RBI cut rates. PREMISE: Rate cuts improve bank NIM. INFERENCE (Deductive): HDFC Bank NIM will improve next quarter. CONFIDENCE: 0.72" |
| **Anti-Examples** | "Stock went up" is an observation, not an inference; "Stock will go up because it always does" is a fallacy masquerading as an inference |
| **Complexity** | Medium to High — multi-step inferences have compounding uncertainty |
| **Evolution Over Time** | Inference quality improves as base rates are better calibrated; new inference patterns are discovered |

### PRIM-006 — BELIEF

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Primitive Code** | PRIM-006 |
| **Name** | Belief |
| **Definition** | A proposition held by the system to be true with a quantified degree of confidence — the basic cognitive state that represents the system's view of the world |
| **Why It Exists** | Actions require beliefs. Before the system can decide to buy, sell, or hold, it must believe something about the likely future. Beliefs are the cognitive states that bridge evidence (what we observe) and decisions (what we do). The entire epistemic architecture exists to produce well-justified beliefs. |
| **Classification** | Epistemic | Cognitive State | Central |
| **Inputs Required** | Evidence (PRIM-002); Inferences (PRIM-005); Prior beliefs; Knowledge items |
| **Outputs Produced** | A structured belief record: the proposition, the confidence level, the evidence base, the reasoning trace |
| **Producers** | Evidence integration engine; inference engine; learning system |
| **Consumers** | Conviction calculation; decision engine; multi-agent debate; learning feedback |
| **Quality Dimensions** | Justification quality (how well-supported is it?); Calibration (does confidence match accuracy?); Coherence (is it consistent with other beliefs?); Currency (how recent is the evidence?) |
| **Failure Modes** | Unjustified belief (held without evidence); miscalibrated belief (wrong confidence level); incoherent belief (contradicts other held beliefs); stale belief (evidence has expired) |
| **Strengths** | The organizing cognitive state; enables systematic updating; provides the foundation for decision |
| **Weaknesses** | Prone to anchoring and confirmation bias; can be held too long in the face of contradicting evidence |
| **Dependencies** | Evidence infrastructure; prior probability framework; coherence maintenance system |
| **Relationships to Other Primitives** | Built from → Evidence (PRIM-002) + Inference (PRIM-005); Feeds into → Conviction (PRIM-007); Resolved by → Decision (PRIM-009); Updated by → Learning (PRIM-018) |

### PRIM-007 — CONFIDENCE

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Primitive Code** | PRIM-007 |
| **Name** | Confidence |
| **Definition** | The system's quantified estimate of the probability that a specific belief, hypothesis, or inference is correct — a number from 0 to 1 representing the degree of epistemic certainty |
| **Why It Exists** | Not all beliefs are equally well-supported. Confidence is the mechanism by which the system represents the varying quality of its beliefs — and acts proportionally to that quality. Without explicit confidence levels, the system cannot distinguish well-supported beliefs from guesses. |
| **Classification** | Quantitative | Epistemic | Continuous |
| **Inputs Required** | The evidence pool for the belief; source reliability scores; evidence independence assessment; prior probability |
| **Outputs Produced** | A probability value [0, 1]; a confidence interval; a breakdown of the contributing evidence items |
| **Producers** | Conviction calculator; Bayesian update engine; evidence weighting model |
| **Consumers** | Conviction (PRIM-008); Decision engine; Position sizing model; Risk management |
| **Quality Dimensions** | Calibration (accuracy of the confidence estimate); Resolution (ability to distinguish between levels); Reliability (consistency across time and contexts) |
| **Failure Modes** | Overconfidence (confidence > warranted by evidence); Underconfidence (confidence < warranted by evidence); False precision (expressing 0.736 when 0.7 is the real accuracy) |
| **Strengths** | Quantifies uncertainty explicitly; enables proportional action; enables calibration improvement |
| **Weaknesses** | Difficult to set correctly; susceptible to systematic biases; can create false sense of precision |
| **Dependencies** | Evidence quality assessments; source reliability database; base rate library; Bayesian update framework |
| **Relationships to Other Primitives** | Property of → Belief (PRIM-006), Hypothesis (PRIM-004), Inference (PRIM-005); Feeds → Conviction (PRIM-008); Updated by → Evidence (PRIM-002) |
| **Lifecycle** | Prior initialized from base rates → Evidence arrives → Bayesian update → Confidence revised → Coherence checked → Decay applied if no new evidence → Updated again on new evidence |
| **Validation Methods** | Calibration curves (actual accuracy at each stated confidence level); Brier Score; Log loss |
| **Confidence Requirements** | Meta-level: the system must track its calibration accuracy; the confidence in confidence must itself be calibrated |
| **State Changes When Updated** | Belief record: confidence value updated; Conviction score: recalculates; Threshold check: performed |
| **Temporal Properties** | Confidence decays toward the prior as evidence ages; time-sensitive evidence ages faster |
| **Scope** | Applied to every belief, hypothesis, and inference in the system |
| **Reversibility** | Confidence values are continuously updated; historical values are archived |
| **Importance to System** | Critical — the quantitative backbone of all Bayesian reasoning |
| **Risk If Missing** | All beliefs are treated equally regardless of evidence quality; system cannot distinguish signal from noise |
| **Examples** | "Conviction: 0.67 — based on 3 supporting signals (earnings beat 0.85, NIM expansion 0.72, analyst upgrade 0.60) and 1 contradicting signal (insider selling 0.75)"; "Prior: 0.45 (base rate for sector outperformance in current regime)" |
| **Anti-Examples** | "I'm fairly confident" — not quantified; "Definitely will happen" — overconfident, unquantified |
| **Complexity** | Medium — calculation is well-defined; calibration requires extensive historical data |
| **Evolution Over Time** | Calibration improves with more outcomes; confidence calculation model becomes more sophisticated |

| **Validation Methods** | Evidence independence check; threshold calibration against historical win rates; contradiction confirmation |

| **Reversibility** | Conviction can be withdrawn if new contradicting evidence arrives before execution |
| **Importance to System** | Critical — the gate between analysis and action; the most important quality control checkpoint |
| **Risk If Missing** | System acts on weak hypotheses; win rate falls; capital is wasted on low-quality ideas |

| **Anti-Examples** | "Conviction based on 5 news articles from the same reporter" — dependent evidence; not valid conviction |

| **Evolution Over Time** | Threshold calibrated to optimize risk-adjusted win rate; evidence weighting improves with learning |

| **Failure Modes** | Undetected contradiction; ignored contradiction; false contradiction (apparent but not real); resolved incorrectly |
| **Lifecycle** | Evidence conflict detected → Contradiction registered → Severity assessed → Resolution attempted → Resolved (one evidence item revised or discarded) OR Persists (conviction reduced, uncertainty flagged) → Archived |
| **Examples** | "CONTRADICTION: Evidence A: Promoter bought 0.5% stake (bullish signal, confidence 0.80). Evidence B: CFO sold shares same week (bearish signal, confidence 0.75). RESOLUTION: Required before conviction maintained. INVESTIGATION: Different timeframes? Different information? Circular trading pattern?" |
| **Importance to System** | Critical — the highest-priority diagnostic event in reasoning |
| **Constitutional Status** | CANNOT be ignored; must be resolved or explicitly acknowledged and penalized |

### PRIM-017 — CONSENSUS

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Primitive Code** | PRIM-017 |
| **Name** | Consensus |
| **Definition** | A state in which all or most reasoning agents have reached the same conclusion through independent analysis — representing collective agreement after debate |
| **Why It Exists** | When multiple agents with different analytical frameworks, different evidence specializations, and different cognitive styles reach the same conclusion independently, the conclusion is more likely to be correct. Consensus is a quality signal — but only if the agents were genuinely independent. |
| **Classification** | Multi-Agent | Agreement State | Quality Signal |
| **Inputs Required** | Multi-agent debate outputs; voting mechanism; independence verification |
| **Outputs Produced** | A consensus declaration: the agreed conclusion, the voting record, the dissenting opinions if any, the independence verification |
| **Quality Dimensions** | Genuine independence of agents (key quality requirement); absence of groupthink; strength of the underlying arguments |
| **Failure Modes** | False consensus from groupthink; consensus without independent analysis; unanimous consensus that should be questioned |
| **Relationships** | Produced by → Multi-agent debate; Boosts → Conviction (PRIM-008); Must not suppress → Minority Opinion |
| **Examples** | "3 of 5 agents: Bull. 1 agent: Neutral. 1 agent: Bear (devil's advocate). Consensus: BULL (weighted 4.1/5). Dissent from devil's advocate recorded." |
| **Constitutional Rule** | Unanimous consensus (5/5) is a red flag — triggers automatic contrarian review |
| **Importance to System** | High — but requires active protection against groupthink |

---

### PRIM-018 — LEARNING RECORD

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Primitive Code** | PRIM-018 |
| **Name** | Learning Record |
| **Definition** | A permanent, structured record of a completed decision cycle — capturing the original hypothesis, the evidence used, the conviction score, the decision made, the actual outcome, and the specific lessons derived |
| **Why It Exists** | Intelligence without learning is static. The learning record is the mechanism by which every decision outcome — whether success or failure — is converted into system improvement. Without learning records, the system cannot distinguish skill from luck, or identify systematic errors in its reasoning. |
| **Classification** | Persistent | Evolutionary | Quality Improvement |
| **Inputs Required** | Completed trade outcome; original hypothesis; evidence used; conviction score; decision record |
| **Outputs Produced** | A structured learning entry: outcome, prediction accuracy, evidence quality assessment, reasoning quality assessment, specific improvement recommendations |
| **Producers** | Learning System (Layer 13); automated on every trade close |
| **Consumers** | Strategy performance tracker; knowledge base (creates or updates knowledge items); calibration system; strategy weight adjuster |
| **Quality Dimensions** | Completeness (all relevant factors captured?); Attribution accuracy (was the right cause identified?); Actionability (does it produce specific improvements?) |
| **Failure Modes** | Outcome bias (judging reasoning quality by outcome, not process); attribution error (blaming market when system was wrong) |
| **Lifecycle** | Trade closes → Outcome recorded → Prediction compared to outcome → Evidence quality retrospectively assessed → Reasoning quality assessed → Specific lessons extracted → Knowledge base updated → Strategy weight adjusted |
| **Examples** | "Learning Record TR-2026-0431: TATAMOTORS LONG. Entry ₹920, Stop hit ₹879. Loss: -4.5%. Prediction was: +8% within 30 days. ACTUAL: -4.5% in 12 days. Root cause: Ignored contradicting evidence (FX headwind). Lesson: FX exposure must be explicitly modeled for auto exporters. Evidence type weight update: FX evidence +15% in auto sector." |
| **Importance to System** | Critical — the primary mechanism of system evolution and improvement |
| **Constitutional Status** | Every trade outcome, every signal outcome, every prediction outcome must produce a learning record |

---

### PRIM-019 — PRIOR

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Primitive Code** | PRIM-019 |
| **Name** | Prior |
| **Definition** | The probability assigned to a hypothesis or outcome before any case-specific evidence is considered — derived from historical base rates and structural knowledge |
| **Why It Exists** | Every reasoning chain must start somewhere. The prior is the epistemically honest starting point — it represents what the system believes based on historical frequency alone, before being influenced by case-specific evidence. Starting from a calibrated prior prevents anchoring on arbitrary starting points. |
| **Classification** | Bayesian | Starting Point | Historical |
| **Inputs Required** | Historical frequency data; reference class definition; regime-conditional base rates |
| **Outputs Produced** | A probability value [0, 1] representing the historical frequency of the outcome in the reference class |
| **Producers** | Base rate library; historical data analysis; regime classifier |
| **Consumers** | Bayesian update engine; confidence calculator; prediction models |
| **Quality Dimensions** | Reference class accuracy (is the right comparison class used?); Sample size sufficiency; Regime conditioning (is the prior regime-specific?) |
| **Failure Modes** | Wrong reference class; ignoring the prior (base rate neglect); over-adjusting from the prior on single pieces of vivid evidence |
| **Examples** | "Prior for large-cap banking sector beat: 0.55 (historical rate in current market regime)"; "Prior for market correction >10% in next 30 days: 0.12 (historical frequency in low-volatility regimes)" |
| **Importance to System** | High — the Bayesian anchor; prevents reasoning from vivid but misleading single cases |
| **Constitutional Rule** | Every hypothesis must start with a calibrated prior; prior cannot be assumed to be 0.5 without evidence |

---

### PRIM-020 — POSTERIOR

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Primitive Code** | PRIM-020 |
| **Name** | Posterior |
| **Definition** | The updated probability of a hypothesis after applying all available case-specific evidence to the prior using Bayes' Theorem — the current best estimate given everything the system knows |
| **Why It Exists** | Evidence matters. The posterior is the mechanism by which the system moves from "what generally happens" (prior) to "what is likely in this specific case given all current evidence" (posterior). It is the quantitative expression of how much the evidence has changed the system's view. |
| **Classification** | Bayesian | Updated | Operative |
| **Inputs Required** | Prior (PRIM-019); all relevant evidence items with their likelihood ratios; evidence independence assessment |
| **Outputs Produced** | Updated probability estimate; a record of how each evidence item moved the probability |
| **Producers** | Bayesian update engine |
| **Consumers** | Conviction calculator; decision engine; prediction models; learning (comparison to outcome) |
| **Quality Dimensions** | Update completeness (was all evidence included?); Correct update direction (did supports raise, contradictions lower?); Independence handling (was double-counting avoided?) |
| **Failure Modes** | Ignoring contradicting evidence; double-counting dependent evidence; over-updating on single vivid evidence; under-updating on strong statistical evidence |
| **Examples** | "HDFC Beat Hypothesis Posterior: Prior: 0.55. After earnings beat (+): 0.72. After NIM guidance (+): 0.80. After analyst upgrade (+): 0.84. After promoter selling (-): 0.76. Posterior: 0.76." |
| **Importance to System** | Critical — the current operative probability; the input to conviction and decision |
| **Constitutional Rule** | The posterior must update in both directions — supporting evidence raises it; contradicting evidence lowers it. Refusing to lower on contradiction is a failure mode. |

### RT-01 — DEDUCTIVE REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Reasoning from general principles to specific conclusions — if the premises are true and the argument is valid, the conclusion is guaranteed to be true |
| **Core Mechanism** | Major Premise + Minor Premise → Certain Conclusion |
| **Strengths** | Certainty-preserving; completely reliable if premises are sound; traceable; the most defensible form |
| **Weaknesses** | Completely dependent on premise quality; cannot go beyond what premises contain; no new knowledge generated |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Applying known earnings patterns to specific companies; risk rule enforcement; known regulatory impact analysis |
| **Failure Modes** | False premise (the general rule doesn't apply here); invalid argument form; hidden assumptions in premises |
| **Example** | "P1: Companies with sustained ROIC > WACC create shareholder value (general principle). P2: HDFC Bank has ROIC > WACC for 12 consecutive years. CONCLUSION: HDFC has been creating shareholder value for 12 years." |
| **When to Use** | When rules are well-established and their applicability to the current case is certain |
| **Combination** | Combine with Inductive (RT-02) to validate the premises; with Bayesian (RT-04) to quantify confidence |

---

### RT-02 — INDUCTIVE REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Reasoning from specific observations to general principles — the more observations, the stronger the inferred principle, but certainty is never guaranteed |
| **Core Mechanism** | N Specific Observations → Probable General Principle |
| **Strengths** | Generates new knowledge from observations; can discover unexpected patterns; scales with data volume |
| **Weaknesses** | Never produces certainty; subject to overfitting; the next case could always be different |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Strategy discovery from historical backtesting; knowledge item creation; factor identification |
| **Failure Modes** | Overfitting (too specific to historical data); insufficient sample size; non-stationarity (regime change); data snooping |
| **Example** | "OBSERVATIONS: NIFTY fell >5% in 8 of the last 10 occurrences of VIX > 30. INDUCED PRINCIPLE: VIX > 30 is associated with >5% probability of 5%+ NIFTY fall." |
| **When to Use** | For discovering patterns from historical data; for building the knowledge base |
| **Combination** | Combine with Statistical (RT-06) to validate significance; with Walk-Forward testing to prevent overfitting |

---

### RT-03 — ABDUCTIVE REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Reasoning to the best explanation — given observed facts, what is the most plausible explanation? Does not guarantee correctness but finds the most parsimonious explanation |
| **Core Mechanism** | Observation → Best Available Explanation |
| **Strengths** | Works under incomplete information; generates explanations quickly; often produces the correct diagnosis |
| **Weaknesses** | Multiple explanations may fit; "best" is judgment-dependent; can miss the true explanation if imagination is limited |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Explaining unusual volume; diagnosing unexpected price moves; understanding anomalies; M&A detection |
| **Failure Modes** | Satisficing on an incorrect explanation; narrative bias making wrong explanation feel compelling |
| **Example** | "OBSERVATION: Stock rises 8% on very high volume with no public news. ABDUCTION: Best explanation is informed buying ahead of corporate announcement. Investigation initiated." |
| **When to Use** | When explaining anomalies; when incomplete information requires best-guess explanation |
| **Combination** | Always combine with investigation to confirm (deductive test of the abduced explanation) |

---

### RT-04 — BAYESIAN REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Updating probability estimates systematically using Bayes' Theorem — Posterior = (Prior × Likelihood) / Evidence Probability |
| **Core Mechanism** | Prior + Evidence → Posterior via Bayes' Theorem |
| **Strengths** | Principled; coherent; the only mathematically correct way to update beliefs; handles incomplete information naturally |
| **Weaknesses** | Requires calibrated priors; computationally complex with many variables; subjective priors can bias results |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Updating conviction on every new evidence item; computing hypothesis probabilities; calibrating confidence |
| **Failure Modes** | Wrong prior; misspecified likelihood model; ignoring contradicting evidence in update |
| **Example** | "Prior: HDFC beats consensus 55% of time. Likelihood ratio of NIM expansion for beat: 2.8. Posterior: 0.55 × 2.8 / (0.55×2.8 + 0.45×1) = 0.77" |
| **When to Use** | Continuously — this is the primary belief-update mechanism |
| **Constitutional Status** | The dominant reasoning framework for conviction calculation |

---

### RT-05 — PROBABILISTIC REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Reasoning with explicit probability distributions over multiple possible outcomes — representing the full range of uncertainty rather than a single point estimate |
| **Core Mechanism** | Probability Distribution → Decision Under Risk |
| **Strengths** | Captures the full range of outcomes; forces explicit uncertainty acknowledgment; enables expected value calculation |
| **Weaknesses** | Distribution tails are hard to estimate; non-stationary processes violate distributional assumptions |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Scenario probability assignment; expected return calculation; options pricing; position sizing |
| **Failure Modes** | Assuming normality when distributions have fat tails; underestimating tail probabilities; conflating point estimate with full distribution |
| **Example** | "HDFC 12-month return distribution: Normal case +15% (45%), Bull +25% (25%), Bear -8% (20%), Severe Bear -20% (10%). EV = +7.75% at current risk levels" |
| **When to Use** | Whenever quantifying full uncertainty across outcome range |

---

### RT-06 — STATISTICAL REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Using statistical methods to test hypotheses, validate patterns, and distinguish signal from noise in data |
| **Core Mechanism** | Sample Data + Statistical Test → Confidence in Pattern |
| **Strengths** | Objective; replicable; separates signal from noise; controls false discovery rate |
| **Weaknesses** | Only as good as the model; p-values are frequently misunderstood; multiple testing inflates false discovery |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Validating strategy performance; testing factor significance; checking knowledge item reliability |
| **Failure Modes** | p-hacking (multiple testing without correction); insufficient sample; violation of independence assumption |
| **Example** | "Strategy produced 18% CAGR in backtest (p=0.02, Sharpe 1.4, 156 trades). Statistical validation: significant at 5% level. Walk-forward test: Sharpe 0.92 — passes promotion gate" |
| **When to Use** | For validating any claimed pattern before it becomes a knowledge item |

---

### RT-07 — RULE-BASED REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Applying explicit if-then rules to determine actions — the most systematic and reproducible reasoning type |
| **Core Mechanism** | Condition Check → Predetermined Action |
| **Strengths** | Highly consistent; immune to emotional override; fast; auditable |
| **Weaknesses** | Rules may not cover novel situations; edge cases can produce wrong decisions; requires maintenance |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Kill switch triggers; stop loss execution; mandatory contradiction investigation; VIX-based de-risking |
| **Failure Modes** | Rule gap (novel situation not covered); conflicting rules; outdated rules |
| **Example** | "RULE: IF VIX > 45 THEN KILL_SWITCH = TRUE AND all pending buy decisions = CANCELLED. Applied instantly when VIX threshold crossed." |
| **Constitutional Status** | Risk Guardian kill switch is entirely rule-based — no judgment permitted at the kill threshold |

### RT-08 — CASE-BASED REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Solving new problems by finding the most similar past cases in the case library and adapting their solutions |
| **Core Mechanism** | Current Situation → Find Similar Cases → Adapt Solution |
| **Strengths** | Directly uses real-world experience; handles incomplete problem specifications; no need for explicit rules |
| **Weaknesses** | Requires sufficient case library; similarity assessment is subjective; past cases may not generalize |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Regime identification; management quality assessment from track record; historical analogue identification |
| **Failure Modes** | Wrong similarity metric; irrelevant case selected; false analogy |
| **Example** | "Current situation: High inflation + rate hikes + slowing growth. Closest historical case: 2011. 2011 result: NIFTY fell 25% over 12 months; recovery took 18 months. Case-based lesson: Defensive positioning with 18-month horizon" |
| **When to Use** | For regime assessment; for applying management quality knowledge; for novel macro situations |

---

### RT-09 — ANALOGICAL REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Drawing conclusions by identifying structural similarities between a current situation and a well-understood reference case |
| **Core Mechanism** | Source Domain → Structural Mapping → Target Domain Inference |
| **Strengths** | Enables reasoning about novel situations using existing knowledge; often reveals non-obvious insights |
| **Weaknesses** | Analogies always break down at some point; the differences may be more important than the similarities |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Applying lessons from US tech cycles to Indian IT sector; sector development stage analogies |
| **Failure Modes** | False analogy; ignoring key disanalogies; over-extending the analogy |
| **Example** | "Indian UPI payment ecosystem is structurally analogous to early Visa/Mastercard growth phase. Analogy suggests: platform winners compound value over decades as transaction volume grows. Inference: Digital payment stocks are early in their growth trajectory." |
| **When to Use** | For novel situations that resemble well-documented historical cases |

---

### RT-10 — COUNTERFACTUAL REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Reasoning about what would have happened under different conditions — imagining alternative causal histories |
| **Core Mechanism** | Actual Event + Counterfactual Condition → Alternative Outcome |
| **Strengths** | Enables causal inference; critical for learning ("what would have happened if we had held?"); reveals causal structure |
| **Weaknesses** | Counterfactuals cannot be directly observed; can be used to rationalize anything |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Post-trade learning; causal attribution; strategy variant testing |
| **Failure Modes** | Selection bias in which counterfactuals are examined; hindsight contamination |
| **Example** | "TATAMOTORS stop loss hit at ₹879. Counterfactual: if we had ignored FX headwind evidence, we would have sized the position larger. Actual result: -4.5%. Counterfactual result: -6.8%. Lesson: evidence weighting was correct; position sizing was correct." |

### RT-11 — CAUSAL REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Reasoning about cause-and-effect relationships — identifying mechanisms, not just correlations |
| **Core Mechanism** | Evidence of Causal Mechanism → Causal Claim |
| **Strengths** | Produces genuine understanding; enables prediction; survives regime changes better than pure correlation |
| **Weaknesses** | Causal claims are hard to establish; correlation is easy to find, causation is hard to prove |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Understanding why earnings beat occurred; identifying the true drivers of sector performance |
| **Failure Modes** | Confusing correlation with causation; reverse causation; third variable problem |
| **Example** | "CORRELATION: FII flows and NIFTY are correlated (0.72). CAUSAL MECHANISM: FII buying creates mechanical demand → price rises. Reverse causation: NIFTY rising also attracts FII. Both directions are causal." |

### RT-12 — TEMPORAL REASONING

| **Core Mechanism** | Time-Ordered Events → Temporal Relationships → Timing Predictions |
| **Strengths** | Captures the dynamic nature of markets; exploits lead-lag relationships |
| **Weaknesses** | Economic cycles vary in length; lead-lag relationships change |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Cycle positioning; lead indicator interpretation; timing of catalysts |

### RT-13 — SEMANTIC REASONING

| **Weaknesses** | Ambiguous language; translation across contexts |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Management conference call tone analysis; regulatory filing semantic analysis; news sentiment |

### RT-14 — SCENARIO-BASED REASONING

### RT-15 — MONTE CARLO REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|

| **Strengths** | Handles complex interdependencies; produces full outcome distribution; scales to high-dimensional problems |
| **Weaknesses** | Requires good parameter distributions; computationally intensive; garbage-in garbage-out |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Portfolio VaR estimation; stress testing; earnings forecast uncertainty quantification |
| **Example** | "Monte Carlo with 10,000 simulations: Expected portfolio return +12.4%, 95th percentile +28%, 5th percentile -8.2%, VaR (5%) = -8.2%. Position sizing adjusted to keep expected drawdown within 2% daily limit." |

### RT-16 — PORTFOLIO REASONING

| **Strengths** | Ensures macro risk limits are not violated; prevents correlation traps; optimizes capital allocation |
| **Weaknesses** | Correlation estimates are unstable; portfolio effects change non-linearly at the tails |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Position sizing; sector concentration checks; aggregate risk management |
| **Example** | "HDFC individual conviction: 7.2. Portfolio check: banking exposure already 28% of portfolio. Maximum permitted: 30%. Available allocation: 2% only. Position sized to 2% despite 7.2 conviction." |

### RT-17 — MULTI-AGENT REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Synthesizing the outputs of multiple independent specialist reasoning agents through structured debate and weighted aggregation |
| **Core Mechanism** | Agent Specialization + Independent Analysis + Structured Debate → Collective Intelligence |
| **Strengths** | Perspective diversity; bias reduction through disagreement; emergent insight from debate |
| **Weaknesses** | Groupthink if independence is lost; coordination overhead; agent weighting is critical |
| **Investment Use Cases** | High-conviction decision validation; conflict resolution; complex multi-factor situations |
| **Example** | "5 agents: Technical (Bull 0.72), Fundamental (Bull 0.65), Macro (Neutral 0.52), Risk (Concern 0.45), Sentiment (Bull 0.68). Weighted aggregate: 0.61 Bull. Devil's advocate raised: credit cycle risk. Conviction reduced from 7.2 to 6.8 after devil's advocate argument." |

### RT-18 — RISK REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Reasoning that integrates all identified risks — market risk, liquidity risk, execution risk, regime risk, tail risk, and model risk — into a unified risk-adjusted evaluation |

| **Strengths** | Prevents overconfidence; ensures capital preservation; explicit trade-off between return and risk |
| **Weaknesses** | Risk models fail at the tails; correlation assumptions break down in crises |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Position sizing; kill switch activation; portfolio stress testing |
| **Example** | "TATAMOTORS: Return expectation +12%. Risks: FX (HIGH), cyclical (MEDIUM), execution (LOW). Risk-adjusted expected return: +7.8%. Risk-adjusted conviction: 6.1 (below threshold). Decision: HOLD — build more evidence before entry." |

### RT-19 — HIERARCHICAL REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Reasoning at multiple levels of abstraction simultaneously — from global macro down through sector to company — ensuring lower-level conclusions are consistent with higher-level context |
| **Core Mechanism** | Top-Down Context Setting → Bottom-Up Opportunity Identification → Cross-Level Coherence |
| **Strengths** | Prevents isolated stock analysis from ignoring macro context; systematic and complete |
| **Weaknesses** | Top-down biases can override valid bottom-up signals; adds analytical layers |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Complete investment thesis construction; sector selection; ensuring macro-consistency |
| **Example** | "Level 1 (Global): Risk-off environment developing. Level 2 (Macro): India relatively insulated; domestic consumption story intact. Level 3 (Sector): FMCG defensive; volumes holding. Level 4 (Company): HUL volumes +3%, margin expanding. Conclusion: FMCG safe-haven thesis consistent across all 4 levels — HIGH CONVICTION." |

### RT-20 — GAME-THEORETIC REASONING

|---|---|
| **Definition** | Reasoning about strategic interactions where the outcome for one participant depends on the actions of other intelligent participants |
| **Core Mechanism** | Model Other Participants → Anticipate Their Actions → Optimize Own Response |
| **Strengths** | Prevents naive assumptions about market behavior; enables exploitation of predictable institutional behaviors |
| **Weaknesses** | Other participants are partially unknowable; game theory assumptions (rationality) often violated |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Options market maker behavior; FII flow pattern anticipation; short squeeze detection |
| **Example** | "Reasoning about FII behavior: FIIs are momentum followers in EMs. If NIFTY breaks above 26,000 key level, FII allocation models will mechanically increase India weight → inflow → further rally. Structure long position to benefit from predictable FII response." |

### RT-21 — DIAGNOSTIC REASONING

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| **Definition** | Systematically identifying the root cause of an unexpected event by working backward from the observed outcome |
| **Core Mechanism** | Observed Outcome → Symptom Analysis → Root Cause Identification |
| **Strengths** | Produces genuine understanding of what went wrong; prevents recurring errors; drives learning |
| **Weaknesses** | Post-hoc rationalization risk; multiple causes may exist; root cause may be unobservable |
| **Investment Use Cases** | Stop loss post-mortem; strategy degradation diagnosis; market anomaly explanation |
| **Example** | "OBSERVATION: Strategy underperformed for 3 months despite high conviction signals. DIAGNOSTIC: Signal accuracy unchanged; execution quality unchanged; CAUSE: Regime changed to sideways from trending — momentum signals have lower information in sideways regimes. ROOT CAUSE: Regime detector was not triggering strategy deactivation quickly enough." |

## PART V — THE COMPLETE REASONING PIPELINE

*The full intelligence pipeline — from raw information arriving at the system boundary to knowledge updating after an outcome is observed. Every step is documented, sequenced, and governed.*

---

### Pipeline Overview

```
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
                    THE REASONING PIPELINE
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

LAYER 0 ─── DATA INGESTION
              │
              │  Market data, news, filings, macro data, alt data arrive
              │  at the system boundary via feeds
              │
              ▼
LAYER 1 ─── OBSERVATION REGISTRATION
              │
              │  Data is parsed, timestamped, and registered as observations
              │  Anomaly detection flags unusual observations for priority processing
              │  Routine observations enter standard processing queue
              │
              ▼
LAYER 2 ─── EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION
              │
              │  Observations are evaluated for relevance to active hypotheses
              │  Relevant observations become evidence items
              │  Each evidence item is classified: supports / contradicts / neutral
              │  Each evidence item receives: source reliability × relevance = weight
              │
              ▼
LAYER 3 ─── EVIDENCE VALIDATION
              │
              │  Evidence quality checks: accuracy, completeness, timeliness
              │  Cross-source validation for high-weight evidence
              │  Staleness check: is this evidence current?
              │  Independence check: is this independent from other evidence already held?
              │
              ▼
LAYER 4 ─── HYPOTHESIS MANAGEMENT
              │
              ├── EXISTING HYPOTHESIS: evidence is added to the hypothesis evidence pool
              │     Prior probability → Bayesian update → New posterior
              │     Conviction score recalculated
              │
              └── NEW SIGNAL: if no active hypothesis exists for this entity
                    New hypothesis generated
                    Prior probability initialized from base rates
                    Hypothesis enters active pool
              │
              ▼
LAYER 5 ─── ALTERNATIVE HYPOTHESIS GENERATION
              │
              │  For every hypothesis, generate at least one alternative hypothesis
              │  that could also explain the observed evidence
              │  Alternative hypotheses are held simultaneously
              │  Evidence is evaluated against BOTH primary and alternative hypotheses
              │
              ▼
LAYER 6 ─── CONVICTION CALCULATION
              │
              │  For each active hypothesis:
              │    Sum of evidence weights (supporting items)
              │    minus Sum of evidence weights (contradicting items)
              │    × Independence multiplier (penalize dependent evidence)
              │    × Source diversity multiplier (reward diverse sources)
              │    = Conviction Score [0 to 10]
              │
              │  Conviction Threshold: 6.5 / 10
              │  Below threshold: continue monitoring
              │  At or above threshold: advance to debate
              │
              ▼
LAYER 7 ─── CONTRADICTION DETECTION AND RESOLUTION
              │
              │  Before advancing, full coherence audit:
              │  Are there any unresolved contradictions in the evidence set?
              │  MINOR contradiction: apply penalty, continue
              │  MAJOR contradiction: require resolution before advancing
              │  CRITICAL contradiction: block advancement until resolved
              │
              ▼
LAYER 8 ─── MULTI-AGENT DEBATE
              │
              │  5 specialist agents receive the hypothesis and evidence set
              │    Agent 1 (Technical): evaluates price and volume evidence
              │    Agent 2 (Fundamental): evaluates company economics evidence
              │    Agent 3 (Macro): evaluates macro and sector context evidence
              │    Agent 4 (Risk): evaluates risk factors and failure scenarios
              │    Agent 5 (Sentiment): evaluates behavioral and flow evidence
              │
              │  Each agent:
              │    Reviews evidence relevant to their specialization
              │    Constructs a structured argument
              │    Assigns a directional vote: Bull / Bear / Neutral
              │    Assigns a confidence score to their vote
              │
              │  Devil's Advocate Protocol:
              │    One agent is randomly assigned devil's advocate role
              │    Must construct the STRONGEST POSSIBLE opposing argument
              │    Must cite evidence that the bull case is ignoring
              │
              ▼
LAYER 9 ─── CONFIDENCE ESTIMATION AND AGGREGATION
              │
              │  Agent votes are aggregated with weights:
              │    Each agent weight = Historical accuracy in current regime
              │    × Domain relevance to current evidence type
              │
              │  Agreement score calculated
              │  Contradiction score calculated
              │  Adjusted conviction = Base conviction × Agreement bonus × Contradiction penalty
              │
              ▼
LAYER 10 ── DECISION RECOMMENDATION GENERATION
              │
              │  If adjusted conviction ≥ 6.5:
              │    Decision recommendation generated
              │    Includes: direction, entity, rationale reference, confidence
              │
              │  If adjusted conviction < 6.5:
              │    Hypothesis returns to monitoring
              │    Evidence accumulation continues
              │
              ▼
LAYER 11 ── RISK REVIEW AND APPROVAL
              │
              │  Risk Guardian checks:
              │    Portfolio utilization < 90%?
              │    Daily loss limit not breached?
              │    VIX < kill switch threshold (45)?
              │    Sector concentration within limits?
              │    Position size within conviction-calibrated limits?
              │
              │  APPROVED: advance to execution
              │  REJECTED: reason logged; hypothesis continues monitoring
              │
              ▼
LAYER 12 ── DECISION FORMALIZATION
              │
              │  Decision record created:
              │    Entity, direction, quantity, price parameters
              │    Stop loss level (% below entry for long)
              │    Target level (% above entry for long)
              │    Expected conviction-adjusted hold time
              │    Hypothesis reference
              │    Conviction score at decision time
              │    Timestamp
              │
              ▼
LAYER 13 ── EXECUTION
              │
              │  Order formatted and submitted to Execution Engine
              │  Order type selected: market / limit / SL-order
              │  Execution monitored for slippage
              │  Position opened and confirmed
              │  Trade Monitor activated for this position
              │
              ▼
LAYER 14 ── POSITION MONITORING
              │
              │  Continuous real-time monitoring:
              │    Price vs stop loss → exit if crossed
              │    Price vs target → scale out / close if reached
              │    Thesis validity → ongoing evidence accumulation
              │    Conviction recalculation → if falls below 4.0, exit regardless
              │    Regime check → if regime changes, reassess
              │
              ▼
LAYER 15 ── POSITION CLOSURE
              │
              │  Exit conditions:
              │    Stop loss hit → immediate exit
              │    Target hit → partial or full exit
              │    Thesis invalidation → exit regardless of price
              │    Time expiry → exit if conviction not refreshed
              │
              │  Exit order submitted and confirmed
              │  Final PnL calculated
              │
              ▼
LAYER 16 ── OUTCOME RECORDING
              │
              │  Trade outcome record created:
              │    Entry and exit price, quantity, PnL
              │    Holding period
              │    Strategy attribution
              │    Regime at entry and exit
              │    Evidence items that drove entry
              │
              ▼
LAYER 17 ── LEARNING AND KNOWLEDGE UPDATE
              │
              │  Learning record created (PRIM-018)
              │  Prediction vs outcome compared
              │  Evidence item accuracy updated: each item credited or discounted
              │  Strategy performance metrics updated (win rate, average return)
              │  Knowledge items updated: confirming instances added, disconfirming investigated
              │  Calibration model updated: overconfidence/underconfidence detected and corrected
              │  New knowledge item created if pattern confirmed across enough instances
              │
              ▼
COMPLETE — CYCLE READY FOR NEXT OBSERVATION

════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
```

### Special Pipeline: The Contradiction Resolution Protocol

When a Critical contradiction is detected (supporting evidence and contradicting evidence both with weight > 0.70):

```
CRITICAL CONTRADICTION DETECTED
          │
          ▼
STEP 1: PAUSE — Do NOT advance to debate
          │
          ▼
STEP 2: IDENTIFY — Which two evidence items are in conflict?
          │
          ▼
STEP 3: SOURCE AUDIT — Are both sources reliable?
          │
          ├── One source is low reliability → Discount that evidence; continue
          │
          └── Both sources are high reliability
                    │
                    ▼
          STEP 4: TEMPORAL AUDIT — Are the evidence items from different time periods?
                    │
                    ├── Different periods → Both may be correct; note the change; continue with recent evidence weighted higher
                    │
                    └── Same period → GENUINE CONFLICT
                              │
                              ▼
                    STEP 5: INVESTIGATION
                              │
                              ├── Is there a third explanation that reconciles both?
                              │     (E.g., Promoter buying = long-term view; CFO selling = liquidity need)
                              │
                              ├── Can the conflict be resolved by additional evidence?
                              │     → Trigger urgent investigation request
                              │
                              └── Cannot be resolved
                                        │
                                        ▼
                              STEP 6: BOTH EVIDENCE ITEMS PENALIZED
                              Conviction reduced by 20% for unresolved critical contradiction
                              Hypothesis marked: CONTRADICTED_UNRESOLVED
```

```
STEP 11: FINAL CONVICTION DECLARATION
```

### Conflict Resolution Protocol

| Conflict Type | Definition | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| **Minor Disagreement** | 4 agents Bull, 1 agent Neutral | Proceed with note; Neutral agent's concern appended to decision record |
| **Significant Disagreement** | 3 agents Bull, 2 agents Bear/Neutral | Conviction reduced 10%; minority opinion fully documented |
| **Split Decision** | 2-2-1 across directions | Devil's advocate argument intensified; conviction reduced 20%; final decision deferred |
| **Deadlock** | 2-2 Bull/Bear, no neutral | Chief Intelligence Agent determines tie-breaker direction; conviction capped at 6.0 |
| **Complete Disagreement** | No two agents agree | HYPOTHESIS ARCHIVED; insufficient consensus; return to evidence accumulation |
| **Unanimous Agreement** | 5-0 in any direction | AUTOMATIC SKEPTICISM TRIGGER: Independent meta-agent review mandated |

### The Confidence Hierarchy

```

│  Debate validation completed

│  Standard monitoring
│
LEVEL 2 — LOW CONFIDENCE (0.40 – 0.55)
│  Weak or sparse evidence
│  Hypothesis held provisionally
│  Evidence accumulation ongoing
│  Low priority monitoring
│
LEVEL 1 — PRIOR ONLY (< 0.40)
│  No case-specific evidence
│  Historical base rate only
│  Hypothesis held at prior
│  Waiting for initial signals
│
LEVEL 0 — BELOW THRESHOLD (< 0.10)
   No meaningful evidence
   Hypothesis archived
   Below floor; not actively held
```

### The Conviction Score Formula (Conceptual)

The conviction score aggregates evidence into a single [0, 10] metric. The computation is conceptually structured as follows:

**Step 1 — Evidence Collection**
Collect all evidence items E₁, E₂, ..., Eₙ for the active hypothesis.

**Step 2 — Weight Assignment**
Each evidence item receives a weight based on:
- Source reliability (how often this source is accurate)
- Relevance (how directly it applies to this hypothesis)
- Recency (time-decay factor; evidence aged > T days is discounted)
- Independence (if two items come from the same causal source, they are counted as one)

**Step 3 — Direction Classification**
Each evidence item is classified:
- Supporting (+): raises the probability of the hypothesis being true
- Contradicting (−): lowers the probability
- Neutral (0): no directional impact

**Step 4 — Raw Conviction Calculation**
Raw Conviction = Sum of Supporting Evidence Weights
               − Sum of Contradicting Evidence Weights
               adjusted to [0, 10] scale against maximum theoretical evidence set

**Step 5 — Diversity Bonus**
When evidence comes from multiple independent domains (technical + fundamental + macro + flow + sentiment), a diversity bonus is applied. Maximum diversity = all 5 domains represented.

**Step 6 — Contradiction Penalty**
For each unresolved contradiction above a materiality threshold:
- Minor contradiction: −0.3 penalty
- Major contradiction: −0.7 penalty
- Critical contradiction: −1.5 penalty OR block advancement

**Step 7 — Debate Adjustment**
After multi-agent debate, the conviction score is adjusted by:
- Agreement score (proportion of agents agreeing with direction)
- Devil's advocate reduction (mandatory −0.3 to −0.7 depending on argument quality)

**Step 8 — Final Conviction Score**
Final Conviction = (Raw Conviction + Diversity Bonus − Contradiction Penalties) × Debate Multiplier
Bounded: [0, 10]

### Conviction vs Confidence: The Critical Distinction

| Property | Confidence | Conviction |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Probability that a specific belief is correct | High-confidence belief with multiple independent evidence streams above action threshold |
| **Range** | [0, 1] continuous | [0, 10] composite score |
| **Threshold** | No action threshold | 6.5 / 10 required for decision |
| **Evidence Requirement** | Can be held with single evidence | Requires minimum 3 independent evidence types |
| **Debate Requirement** | Not required | Required before decision recommendation |
| **Decay** | Decays toward prior as evidence ages | Decays as evidence ages and new contradictions arrive |
| **Revision** | Updated on any new evidence | Updated on any new evidence; recalculated after debate |
| **Role** | Input to conviction calculation | Output of the full reasoning pipeline; gate to action |

### Confidence Dynamics

#### Confidence Growth Model

When new supporting evidence arrives:

```
New Confidence = Old Confidence + (Surprise Value × Evidence Weight × (1 − Old Confidence))
```

Explanation:
- Evidence has more impact when it is surprising (Surprise Value is high)
- Evidence has more impact when the source is reliable (Evidence Weight is high)
- Evidence has diminishing impact as confidence approaches 1 (the ceiling effect)

#### Confidence Decay Model

When no new evidence arrives, confidence decays toward the prior:

```
Confidence(t) = Prior + (Confidence(0) − Prior) × e^(−λt)
```

Where:
- λ = decay rate (varies by evidence type; event-driven evidence decays faster than structural evidence)
- t = time elapsed since last evidence update
- Prior = historical base rate for this hypothesis type

**Decay Rate Examples:**
- Price momentum signal: λ = 0.15/day (decays quickly)
- Earnings fundamentals: λ = 0.01/day (decays slowly)
- Macro regime: λ = 0.005/day (decays very slowly)
- Management quality: λ = 0.001/day (very persistent)

### Detailed Failure Mode Analysis

#### FM-01 — OVERCONFIDENCE

**Description:** The system assigns confidence levels that exceed what the available evidence justifies. The system believes it knows more than it actually knows.

**Why It Occurs:**
- Confirmation bias leads to accumulating supporting evidence while dismissing contradicting
- Small sample sizes feel more convincing than they are statistically
- Narrative coherence creates an illusion of certainty
- Recent success increases risk-taking beyond what is warranted

**Detection Methods:**
- Calibration curve analysis: plot stated confidence vs actual accuracy for all predictions; a well-calibrated system's curve is the identity line
- Brier Score: a proper scoring rule that penalizes overconfidence
- Pattern: win rate significantly below stated confidence → overconfidence detected

**Mitigation:**
- Automatic discounting of confidence when sample size < 30 instances
- Mandatory devil's advocate argument before conviction is declared
- Periodic recalibration: confidence scores are adjusted by the ratio of actual accuracy to stated confidence

**Recovery:**
- When overconfidence is detected in a specific domain, apply an overconfidence correction factor for that domain until recalibration
- Learning system prioritizes recalibration events

---

#### FM-02 — HALLUCINATION

**Description:** The reasoning system generates conclusions, facts, or evidence that are not based on actual observations or validated knowledge — essentially fabricating information.

**Why It Occurs:**
- Reasoning fills in gaps with plausible-seeming content
- Pattern completion produces fictional details that feel like memories
- Generalization from partial patterns produces specific claims that are not observed

**Detection Methods:**
- Every conclusion must cite specific evidence items with timestamps and sources
- Any conclusion not backed by a citation is flagged as potential hallucination
- Periodic audit: randomly select conclusions and trace them to their source evidence

**Mitigation:**
- Evidence citation is mandatory for all factual claims in reasoning
- Conclusions are tagged: OBSERVED (direct evidence) vs INFERRED (derived) vs ASSUMED (prior only)
- The system cannot promote an ASSUMED belief to a decision recommendation

**Recovery:**
- When hallucination is detected: the conclusion is invalidated
- The pathway that produced the hallucination is audited
- All decisions based on the hallucinated conclusion are reviewed

---

#### FM-03 — NOISE AMPLIFICATION

**Description:** Random variation in data is misinterpreted as meaningful signal, leading to hypothesis generation and conviction building based on statistical noise.

**Why It Occurs:**
- Human pattern-recognition instinct is applied to random data
- Small samples are mistaken for representative patterns
- Clustering illusion: random clusters feel like intentional patterns

**Detection Methods:**
- Statistical significance test for all claimed patterns: p-value requirement
- Sample size check: minimum observations required before pattern is elevated to knowledge item
- Out-of-sample test: the pattern must appear in data not used to discover it

**Mitigation:**
- All patterns require statistical validation before becoming evidence
- Pattern confidence is explicitly discounted for small samples
- Walk-forward testing required for all knowledge items

**Recovery:**
- When noise amplification is detected: the affected knowledge item is downgraded or retired
- Evidence items derived from the noisy pattern are revisited

---

#### FM-04 — FALSE CORRELATION

**Description:** Two variables that happen to co-move in the historical sample are incorrectly treated as meaningfully related, when the relationship is coincidental.

**Why It Occurs:**
- The universe of potential correlations is enormous; many will appear by chance
- Correlations can be established by a common third variable (confounders)
- Correlations can be driven by the same historical period without fundamental relationship

**Detection Methods:**
- Correlation-causation test: for every claimed correlation, a causal mechanism must be identified
- Out-of-sample test: the correlation must hold in data not used to establish it
- Regime test: the correlation must hold across multiple distinct market regimes

**Mitigation:**
- Correlation alone is never sufficient to create a knowledge item
- "What is the mechanism?" is the mandatory first question for any claimed relationship

**Recovery:**
- When a false correlation is discovered: the derived knowledge items are retired
- All strategies built on the correlation are reviewed

---

#### FM-14 — MODEL DRIFT

**Description:** A model or knowledge item that was accurate in the past becomes progressively less accurate as market conditions change — but the system continues to use it without detecting the degradation.

**Why It Occurs:**
- Market regimes change; statistical relationships that held in one regime may not hold in another
- Technology, regulation, and economic structure evolve over time
- Competitive dynamics change as the strategy is adopted by more participants

**Detection Methods:**
- Rolling window performance monitoring: is the model's accuracy in the last 90 days significantly below its all-time accuracy?
- Regime-conditional performance: is the model performing differently in the current regime vs prior regimes?
- Statistical tests for performance change: has a statistically significant performance break been detected?

**Mitigation:**
- All knowledge items have an automatic review date (every 90 days for high-frequency; every 6 months for structural)
- Regime-conditioning: knowledge items are tagged with the regimes in which they are valid
- Model confidence is automatically reduced when rolling accuracy drops below a threshold

**Recovery:**
- When model drift is detected: the model is flagged for retraining
- All decisions using the model are reviewed
- If retraining is not possible, the model is deactivated until performance is re-established

---

#### FM-15 — REGIME BLINDNESS

**Description:** The system applies reasoning patterns, strategies, or knowledge items that are valid in one market regime to a fundamentally different regime — without recognizing the regime transition.

**Why It Occurs:**
- Regime transitions can be gradual and hard to detect in real time
- The most recent regime was in effect for a long time; the system is more familiar with it
- Overconfidence in current regime continuation

**Detection Methods:**
- Continuous regime monitoring: VIX level, market breadth, trend/choppiness metrics, sector rotation
- Strategy performance monitoring: a sudden drop in multiple strategy win rates simultaneously is a regime change signal
- Explicit regime classifier: regular (at minimum weekly) regime classification

**Mitigation:**
- ALL knowledge items are tagged with valid regime conditions
- Before every decision, the system explicitly checks: "Is the current regime one in which this knowledge item is valid?"
- Strategy weights are automatically adjusted for regime

**Recovery:**
- When regime blindness is confirmed: all active decisions are reviewed against the new regime
- Strategies not validated for the new regime are suspended until re-validated

---

#### FM-17 — CONFIRMATION BIAS

**Description:** The system seeks, weighs, and retains evidence that confirms its existing hypothesis while underweighting, dismissing, or ignoring evidence that contradicts it.

**Why It Occurs:**
- Information search is guided by the hypothesis
- Contradicting evidence requires more cognitive effort to process
- The narrative coherence of the existing hypothesis resists disruption

**Detection Methods:**
- Contradiction seeking protocol: after every evidence accumulation session, the system must specifically search for contradicting evidence
- Evidence asymmetry check: if the ratio of supporting to contradicting evidence is unusually high, investigate whether contradicting evidence is being missed
- Devil's advocate: a separate agent specifically tasked with finding what is being missed

**Mitigation:**
- Mandatory contradiction-seeking step in every reasoning cycle
- Evidence is evaluated against the ALTERNATIVE HYPOTHESIS simultaneously
- The devil's advocate role exists specifically to counteract this bias

**Recovery:**
- When confirmation bias is detected: the evidence set is reassembled without the prior thesis in mind
- All contradicting evidence is reviewed at double the standard weight temporarily

---

#### FM-21 — GROUPTHINK

**Description:** The multi-agent debate system converges to a consensus not because all agents independently reached the same conclusion, but because agents were influenced by the apparent emerging consensus, suppressed dissent to avoid conflict, or failed to reason independently.

**Why It Occurs:**
- Agents observe each other's preliminary signals before analysis is complete
- Agents are influenced by perceived authority of other agents
- The cost of dissent (being wrong in opposition to the group) feels higher than the cost of silent agreement

**Detection Methods:**
- Independence verification: were agents' draft arguments prepared before hearing others?
- Unanimous consensus trigger: 5/5 agreement triggers automatic skepticism review
- Minority opinion quality: are minority opinions substantively argued or just nominal?

**Mitigation:**
- All agent analyses are prepared independently before any sharing
- No agent sees another agent's vote until all votes are cast
- The devil's advocate is explicitly required to disagree, regardless of personal view
- Unanimous consensus is treated as a warning sign, not a reassuring signal

**Recovery:**
- When groupthink is detected: the debate is reconvened with new evidence only
- A new devil's advocate is assigned from outside the original debate
- Conviction is reduced by 30% until a genuine dissenting argument is developed

## PART IX — REASONING CONSTITUTION

*Twenty-five constitutional principles governing all reasoning in the Investment Intelligence Operating System. These principles are not guidelines — they are invariant requirements. Violations are tracked and require remediation.*

---

**Principle 1 — Reasoning Must Be Explainable**

Every conclusion, belief, prediction, and decision produced by the system must be fully traceable from the evidence and reasoning steps that produced it. "The model said so" is never an acceptable justification. Explainability is not a feature — it is a constitutional requirement. Any conclusion that cannot be traced to its reasoning chain is invalid.

---

**Principle 2 — Reasoning Must Be Reproducible**

Given the same evidence set, the reasoning process must produce the same or consistent conclusions. If the same evidence produces radically different conclusions on different occasions, the reasoning process has an unacceptable variance that must be diagnosed and corrected.

---

**Principle 3 — Evidence Precedes Belief**

No belief may be elevated above the prior level without supporting evidence. Beliefs are not created by desire, intuition, or narrative coherence alone. The sequence is always: Evidence → Inference → Belief. A belief without traceable evidence is a prior, not a justified belief.

---

**Principle 4 — Contradictions Cannot Be Ignored**

When two evidence items are mutually inconsistent, this conflict cannot be resolved by selecting one and ignoring the other without explicit justification. Every contradiction must be registered, investigated, and resolved or acknowledged as unresolved (with an appropriate conviction reduction). Ignoring contradictions is not just a reasoning error — it is a constitutional violation.

---

**Principle 5 — Confidence Is Earned, Not Assumed**

The system does not start with high confidence and reduce it when problems are found. The system starts with the calibrated prior and increases confidence only as evidence accumulates. High confidence is the result of strong evidence, not the default state. Confidence must be earned through evidence, not assigned by desire.

---

**Principle 6 — Conviction Requires Multiple Independent Evidence Streams**

A single piece of evidence, regardless of its weight, cannot produce conviction. The action threshold (6.5/10) requires contribution from at least 3 independent evidence types. Independent means causally independent — evidence from the same causal source counts once, not multiple times. This principle prevents action based on a single vivid but potentially false signal.

---

**Principle 7 — Every Conclusion Must Be Falsifiable**

All investment theses, predictions, and beliefs must be stated in a form that could in principle be proven wrong. "HDFC is a great company" is not falsifiable and therefore not a useful belief. "HDFC will beat Q4 consensus EPS by >5%" is falsifiable and therefore a useful prediction. If a belief cannot be stated in falsifiable form, it cannot be evaluated or used in the reasoning pipeline.

---

**Principle 8 — Reasoning Must Survive New Information**

A well-reasoned conclusion should be robust to new information that is consistent with its assumptions. A conclusion that collapses the moment any new information arrives was never well-reasoned. Genuine conviction should update gracefully — new evidence refines it, it does not demolish it unless the new evidence is specifically targeted at a key assumption.

---

**Principle 9 — Uncertainty Must Be Explicitly Acknowledged**

Suppressing, hiding, or ignoring uncertainty is a constitutional violation. The system must always state what it does not know alongside what it believes. A high-confidence conclusion with no acknowledged uncertainty is almost certainly overconfident. Uncertainty is not weakness — it is epistemic honesty.

---

**Principle 10 — Calibration Is a Constitutional Requirement**

Confidence levels must match historical accuracy. A system that says "70% confidence" must be right approximately 70% of the time across all statements at that confidence level. Calibration is measured, tracked, and improved continuously. Systematic miscalibration is treated as a reasoning failure, not an acceptable performance variation.

---

**Principle 11 — The Prior Is Always the Starting Point**

No hypothesis may begin its probability assessment above its historical base rate without specific evidence. The prior is the epistemically honest starting point. "This time feels different" is not sufficient to justify starting above the base rate. The Bayesian update process from the prior is the correct framework.

---

**Principle 12 — The Alternative Hypothesis Must Always Be Held**

For every active investment hypothesis, at least one alternative hypothesis that could also explain the available evidence must be explicitly maintained. The system must evaluate evidence against both the primary and alternative hypotheses simultaneously. This prevents premature closure on a single explanation.

---

**Principle 13 — Learning Is Mandatory, Not Optional**

Every decision outcome — whether profitable or unprofitable — must generate a learning record. The system cannot selectively learn only from successes or only from certain failure types. Complete, honest, attribution-correct learning is constitutionally required. A system that does not learn from all outcomes is a system that does not improve.

---

**Principle 14 — The Process Is More Important Than the Outcome**

A decision made with an excellent reasoning process that produces a bad outcome should be rated as a high-quality decision. A decision made with a poor reasoning process that produces a profitable outcome should be rated as a low-quality decision. The learning system must evaluate process quality, not only outcome. This prevents the system from inadvertently learning to reason poorly in ways that happen to have historically succeeded.

---

**Principle 15 — Context Determines Meaning**

No piece of evidence is meaningful without context. The same earnings beat means different things in a bull market versus a bear market. The same VIX level means different things before versus after a major geopolitical event. Every evidence evaluation must explicitly state the context in which it is being evaluated, and this context must be reflected in the evidence weight.

---

**Principle 16 — Biases Must Be Actively Hunted, Not Passively Avoided**

The system does not assume it is unbiased. All known cognitive biases are treated as active threats that require deliberate countermeasures. The contradiction-seeking protocol, the devil's advocate requirement, the independent analysis requirement — these are not optional enhancements. They are constitutional defenses against systematically occurring biases.

---

**Principle 17 — No Belief May Be Held at 100% Confidence**

Absolute certainty is epistemologically unjustifiable in any domain involving empirical facts and forward-looking prediction. The system's confidence ceiling is 0.99. A belief held at 100% confidence is a dogma, not a belief — it is immune to revision and therefore immune to correction when wrong. The 1% residual is the system's acknowledgment of irreducible uncertainty.

---

**Principle 18 — Every AI System Action Must Be an Auditable Event**

Every internal system action — every signal generated, every hypothesis created, every conviction score calculated, every decision recommended, every order submitted — is an event that must be permanently recorded in the audit log. There are no private actions. Everything the system does must be examinable, explainable, and auditable by design.

---

**Principle 19 — Regime Context Is Non-Negotiable**

Before applying any knowledge item, pattern, or strategy, the system must verify that the current market regime is one in which that knowledge item has demonstrated validity. Applying a strategy validated in trending regimes to a sideways market without regime adjustment is a constitutional violation. Regime context is the highest-priority context — it supersedes all other contextual factors.

---

**Principle 20 — The Reasoning Pipeline Has No Shortcuts**

The full pipeline — from evidence through hypothesis through conviction through debate through risk review through decision — cannot be bypassed for speed or convenience. Partial pipeline execution produces lower-quality decisions that expose the system to systematic failures. A decision made without completing the pipeline is unauthorized and must be flagged as a protocol violation.

---

**Principle 21 — Complexity Must Be Justified**

A complex reasoning chain is not inherently better than a simple one. The parsimony principle applies: the simplest reasoning chain that adequately explains the evidence and produces a defensible conclusion is preferred. Complexity that does not improve the quality of the conclusion creates brittleness, obscures reasoning, and increases the probability of error propagation.

---

**Principle 22 — The Minority Opinion Has Equal Standing**

In the multi-agent debate, the minority opinion is not defeated by the majority vote — it is outvoted. The minority argument must be formally recorded, must be monitored for subsequent vindication, and must be acknowledged as a legitimate alternative view. History is full of cases where the minority was right. The system treats minority opinions as intellectual assets, not as noise.

---

**Principle 23 — Time Is a First-Class Dimension of Reasoning**

Every belief, hypothesis, evidence item, and prediction must have an explicit temporal dimension: when was the evidence valid? what is the prediction's horizon? how has the situation evolved over time? Timeless beliefs ("HDFC is always a safe hold") are not permitted. Everything in the system is time-stamped and time-conditioned.

---

**Principle 24 — The Kill Switch Is Inviolable**

When the Risk Guardian's kill switch conditions are met (VIX > 45, daily portfolio loss > 2%), no reasoning process — however compelling, however high-conviction — may override the kill switch. The kill switch is a constitutional hard limit, not a default that can be overridden by sufficiently high conviction. The purpose of the kill switch is to prevent the compounding of errors in extreme conditions — exactly the conditions where reasoning confidence is most likely to be overconfident.

---

**Principle 25 — The System Must Know What It Does Not Know**

The most important failure mode is confident reasoning about areas where the system has no genuine competence or evidence. The system must maintain an explicit knowledge gap register — a record of questions it cannot reliably answer. In those areas, the system must acknowledge its incompetence and defer to the prior, rather than generating high-confidence answers from thin evidence. Epistemic humility about knowledge gaps is the highest form of intelligence.
